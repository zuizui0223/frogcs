# Ecological argument spine v0.3 — multi-day rainfall pulse and community reassembly

## Working title

**Recent rainfall predicts week-long richness elevation and species-selective reassembly in active frog communities**

## Biological question

Rainfall-driven frog calling is established. The paper now asks two ecological questions:

1. **How long does the active-community response remain detectable after rain?**
2. **Does that response simply amplify the same assemblage, or change which species constitute the active community?**

## Claim 1 — the richness signal persists beyond the rain event

Prespecified route × seasonal-window fixed-effects analysis, reference = >=8 days since rain:

- day 0: **+0.654 species**, 95% CI 0.448–0.860;
- day 1: **+0.544**, 95% CI 0.350–0.739;
- days 2–3: **+0.411**, 95% CI 0.227–0.596;
- days 4–7: **+0.381**, 95% CI 0.202–0.561.

All four recent-rain bins are positive; the prespecified pulse endpoint is **days 4–7**.

Exact-consecutive-year dry-anchor sensitivity:
- day 0: +0.866, CI 0.108–1.624;
- day 1: +0.450, CI 0.109–0.790;
- days 2–3: +0.416, CI 0.091–0.741;
- days 4–7: +0.257, CI -0.197–0.710.

Thus the strongest statement is **week-scale support in the route-season fixed-effects curve, with stricter matched support through 2–3 days and directional but imprecise support at 4–7 days**.

## Claim 2 — matched wet conditions contain richer active assemblages

4,236 wet–dry pairs / 585 routes / 21 states:
- richness-gain beta = **+0.2859 species per unit log-rain contrast**;
- 95% CI 0.1646–0.4072;
- P = 3.81e-6.

Exact consecutive-year:
- beta = +0.3009;
- P = 1.60e-4.

## Claim 3 — richness gain includes compositional replacement

Nestedness:
- primary P=.279;
- exact consecutive P=.711.

Turnover:
- primary beta=+.01264, P=.104;
- exact consecutive beta=**+.01840**, P=.00850.

Therefore the wet assemblage is not simply a nested extension of the dry assemblage.

## Claim 4 — reassembly is species-selective at finer than family scale

Adjusted species family:
- Q=**144.01**, df=28, P=1.46e-17;
- raw vs adjusted ranking rho=.997.

Family decomposition:
- within-family Q=**73.88**, df=21, P=8.28e-8;
- 52.1% of family-matched Q remains within families;
- Hylidae P=2.12e-5;
- Ranidae P=5.65e-4.

## Mechanism remains unresolved — and is now bounded

Unsupported/simple explanations:
- pooled body-size pattern does not survive family-stratified permutation;
- coarse ATraiU hydroperiod flags have no usable variation;
- breeding-season breadth P=.823;
- early-period recurrence P=.698;
- early-period seasonal concentration P=.990;
- route historical dryness interaction P=.855;
- dry–dry compositional-memory comparison fails its frozen sample-size gate.

These negative follow-ups should not dominate the paper. Their role is to show that the core ecological pattern is not trivially reducible to the simplest trait/context explanations tested.

## Ecological synthesis

> **Recent rainfall is associated with a multi-day expansion of frog active-community richness and species-selective reassembly. The richness signal remains detectable 4–7 days after reported rain, well beyond an immediate chorus response.**

## Hard boundaries

Do not claim:
- occupancy / abundance change;
- colonization / extinction;
- causal rainfall effects;
- breeding success;
- physiological recovery time or rainfall-response half-life;
- week-long persistence of compositional turnover;
- identified trait mechanism.

Use:
- multi-day rainfall pulse;
- active-community richness;
- species-selective reassembly;
- behaviourally realized community;
- rain-recency class;
- temporal beta diversity.
