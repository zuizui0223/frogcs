# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.3

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Recent rainfall predicts broader frog acoustic participation without stronger residual co-calling associations,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling and calling-species richness are well established. Our study instead asks where a rainfall-associated increase in multispecies detections enters the community observation process: through broader participation across standardized sampling units, through greater species multiplicity inside already-active units, or through stronger residual co-calling after marginal activity is accounted for.

The North American Amphibian Monitoring Program allows these components to be separated. Recent rainfall was associated with a greater probability that standardized 5-min stops contained any caller and with a broader run-level active species pool. In contrast, rainfall did not detectably increase multispecies calling conditional on acoustic activity, observed co-calling relative to a plug-in independence expectation, a fixed-marginal shuffle residual, mean pairwise excess covariance, or prespecified pairwise network density. The rain coefficient also remained negative after temperature and nonlinear seasonal adjustment.

Australian FrogID provides a deliberately different contrast. FrogID recordings are already conditioned on at least one calling species, and recent rainfall strongly predicted multiple rather than single callers within those active recordings. The closest NAAMP activity-conditioned estimator was null. We therefore do not treat FrogID as a direct replication of the NAAMP primary effect; instead, the disagreement shows that the conditional component can depend on biological system, exposure metric, detectability or observation process.

The broader contribution is an empirical warning for community monitoring: multispecies detections can increase because participation expands across sampling units without detectable strengthening of residual species association, and apparently comparable responses can change meaning when observation conditioning changes.

All analyses are observational. Original contract-frozen endpoints are distinguished from reviewer-motivated post-opening diagnostics, and null results, sensitivity limits and conditioning differences are reported explicitly.

Sincerely,

The authors
