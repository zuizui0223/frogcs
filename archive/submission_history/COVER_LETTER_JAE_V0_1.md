# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Recent rainfall predicts greater short-window co-calling in frog communities across continents,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling are well established, and recent work has shown rainfall-triggered chorus onset. Our study addresses a distinct community-level question: whether a shared environmental pulse changes the probability that multiple species occupy the same brief behavioural window.

We test this prediction in two independent continental monitoring systems. In the North American Amphibian Monitoring Program, recent rainfall was associated with greater multispecies co-calling across standardized 5-min route stops. We then prospectively validated the same directional prediction in expert-verified Australian FrogID recordings linked to ERA5 rainfall, conditioning the validation on at least one frog species already being acoustically active. The association also persisted in within-route and within-weather-cell analyses, showing that static spatial differences in local species pools are insufficient to explain the replicated direction.

Two prespecified negative results constrain the mechanism: rainfall did not detectably amplify co-calling at seasonal shoulders and did not densify the pairwise co-calling network. We therefore interpret the replicated pattern as environmentally elastic short-window temporal overlap consistent with shared environmental activation, not as evidence for interspecific facilitation, pairwise network rewiring or a causal rainfall effect.

We believe the study fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses two contrasting large-scale animal monitoring systems to test a general ecological principle: temporal niche separation can vary transiently with shared environmental cues even when particular pairwise relationships do not measurably reorganize.

The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review. Public source data are cited in the manuscript, and the analysis code, frozen contracts, result receipts and deterministic figures are maintained in a standalone reproducibility repository for archival release.

Sincerely,

The authors
