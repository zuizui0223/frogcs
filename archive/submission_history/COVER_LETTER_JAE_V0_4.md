# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.4

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated richness gains accompany species-selective reassembly of active frog communities,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling are well established. Our study asks a different community-ecology question: when wetter conditions increase acoustic activity, does the same assemblage simply become more active, or does the identity of the active community change?

Using 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys, we matched 4,236 wetter–drier comparisons from the same route and the same seasonal sampling window. Greater rainfall contrast predicted higher active-species richness, and this result persisted in exact consecutive-year comparisons. The richer wet assemblage was not a simple nested extension of the dry assemblage: nestedness did not increase, while turnover increased in the consecutive-year sensitivity.

Species responses were strongly non-uniform. Twenty-nine eligibility-qualified species retained extreme wet-versus-dry heterogeneity after adjustment for temperature difference, survey timing, rainfall-contrast magnitude and year gap. Among 26 species with family assignments, 52.1% of total inverse-variance heterogeneity remained within families, with significant internal heterogeneity in both Hylidae and Ranidae. Thus the result is not a coarse contrast among major frog families.

We interpret this as **species-selective short-term reassembly of the acoustically active community**. The paper does not claim short-term colonization, extinction, occupancy change or rainfall causality. A secondary body-size association was strong across species but failed a family-stratified permutation test, so we explicitly retain the ecological mechanism as unresolved rather than promoting an unsupported trait explanation.

We believe this fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it addresses a general animal-community problem: behaviourally realized communities can change membership over short environmental contrasts, even within the same sites and seasonal windows, on timescales far shorter than changes in regional species pools or occupancy.

The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review, and versioned contracts, receipts and deterministic figures are maintained in the standalone reproducibility repository for archival release.

Sincerely,

The authors
