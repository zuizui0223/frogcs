# Novelty audit v0.9 — RC9 cross-continental taxonomic deepening with North American matrix geometry

## Central advance

The paper separates two processes that are often conflated:

- **community amplification**: the same underlying participants become more active or detectable;
- **community recruitment**: additional species × site participation enters the behaviourally realized community.

Rainfall-sensitive frog calling and rainfall-associated richness are prior art. The novelty is not that rain increases calling.

The strongest supported story is hierarchical:

1. **Cross-continental component:** recent-rain conditions are associated with greater taxonomic depth within already-active acoustic units in both North American NAAMP stops and Australian FrogID recordings.
2. **North American spatial component:** within fixed ten-stop NAAMP routes, the species × stop allocation is more boundary-biased than a magnitude-matched uniform-activation null predicts.
3. **Differentiation component:** NAAMP pairwise Sørensen differentiation remains practically stable within a fixed ±0.025 slope margin.

## Cross-continental external validation

North America, frozen pre-existing result:
- active-stop richness rainfall-contrast β = **+0.0794**;
- 95% CI = **+0.0231 to +0.1357**.

Australia, frozen new endpoint:
- 40,754 FrogID recordings;
- excess richness = species richness - 1;
- β per 1 SD increasing log dry-spell exposure = **-0.0818**;
- 95% CI = **-0.0982 to -0.0654**;
- P = **1.54 × 10^-22**.

Sensitivity:
- recorder-clustered CI = **-0.0949 to -0.0686**;
- within-ERA5-cell β = **-0.0998**, 95% CI **-0.1182 to -0.0815**.

Frozen decision: **STRONG PASS**.

Secondary Australian depth endpoints also agree:
- richness >=3: OR = **0.846**, 95% CI **0.811–0.881**;
- excess richness beyond two: β = **-0.0483**, 95% CI **-0.0600 to -0.0367**.

This matters because the old NAAMP conditional >=2-species OR was null. The common signal is therefore better described as **continuous/deeper taxonomic multiplicity**, not necessarily the first 1-to-2-species threshold.

## North American matrix geometry

Uniform activation already predicts much boundary crossing:
- null mean = **80.9%**;
- 95% null interval = **74.2–87.4%**.

Observed:
- boundary crossing = **92.0%**;
- four-component omnibus P = **.000999** under κ = 1, 2 and 5.

The directional departure is:
- corner expansion: **36.9% observed vs 27.4% null**;
- spatial spread: **15.2% vs 22.9%**;
- taxonomic deepening: **39.9% vs 30.5%**;
- within-core rearrangement: **8.0% vs 19.1%**.

Thus the NAAMP expansion is more taxonomically recruiting and less core-rearranging than uniform amplification predicts.

## General principle

> **Environmental pulses can deepen taxonomic participation within already-active communities across independent continental systems, while standardized spatial data reveal that recruitment can exceed uniform amplification without erasing local differentiation.**

## Boundaries

Authorized:
- cross-continental consistency across North American and Australian monitoring systems;
- active-unit taxonomic deepening as the shared response;
- North American boundary-biased spatial recruitment.

Not authorized:
- worldwide universality;
- pooled US–Australia effect size;
- identical mechanism across continents;
- FrogID replication of fixed species × stop geometry;
- rainfall causality;
- occupancy, abundance or demographic recruitment.

## Preferred one-sentence novelty statement

> **Recent rainfall deepens taxonomic participation within already-active frog assemblages across independent North American and Australian monitoring systems, while fixed North American spatial matrices show that expansion is more boundary-biased than uniform amplification predicts and does not practically homogenize local communities.**
