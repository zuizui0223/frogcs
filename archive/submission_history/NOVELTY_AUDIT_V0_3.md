# Novelty audit v0.4 — participation, conditioning and residual association

## Revised claim boundary

The manuscript does **not** claim that rainfall compresses temporal niches, synchronizes frog communities, or that FrogID replicates the same estimand as NAAMP.

The strongest defensible empirical contribution is:

> **In standardized NAAMP monitoring, recent rainfall is associated with broader acoustic participation across route-stops and a larger active species pool, without detectable strengthening of within-active-stop or residual co-calling association. An activity-conditioned FrogID contrast shows that the conditional multiplicity component can differ across biological or observation systems.**

## What is already established

Rainfall effects on frog calling, calling-species richness and chorus onset are established. Hsu et al. (2006), Xie et al. (2017) and Brodie et al. (2025) therefore rule out novelty claims such as “rain makes more frog species call” or “rain synchronizes frog choruses for the first time.”

The conceptual distinction between shared environmental responses and residual multispecies association is also established outside frog bioacoustics (e.g. Royan et al., 2016; Swallow et al., 2016). The contribution here is empirical decomposition in a large standardized acoustic-monitoring system, not invention of that distinction.

## What the current study adds

1. **Standardized participation decomposition.** NAAMP separates all-stop multispecies calling, any-stop activity, run-level active-pool richness, P(>=2 | >=1 active), pairwise network density, plug-in independence residual, mean pairwise excess covariance and a fixed-marginal shuffle residual.
2. **Empirical localization of the rain signal.** Rain is associated with active-stop coverage and run-level pool breadth, whereas all four within-active/residual association diagnostics are null.
3. **Precision of the negative association result.** The NAAMP activity-conditioned 95% CI (OR 0.959–1.017) excludes the adjusted all-stop OR 0.946. On the probability scale, the rain-direction bound of the independence residual is about 8% of the within-route all-stop coefficient magnitude; a fixed-marginal shuffle gives an almost identical bound. These are descriptive precision comparisons, not formal equivalence tests.
4. **Cross-system conditioning contrast.** FrogID contains only already-active recordings and shows a strong activity-conditioned effect (OR 0.853), whereas the closest NAAMP conditional effect is null. The discrepancy is retained as a result, not hidden as “replication.”
5. **Weather and spatial robustness.** NAAMP rain remains negative after temperature and nonlinear day-of-year adjustment; each system’s native estimand also persists within fixed spatial units.
6. **Transparent evidence hierarchy.** Reviewer-motivated pool, residual and shuffle diagnostics are explicitly post-opening and cannot replace original endpoint decisions.

## Reviewer-safe general contribution

> Multispecies activity can rise because participation broadens across sampling units even when residual association does not detectably strengthen; conditioning on already-active observations can produce a different apparent weather response.

## Phrases to avoid

- cross-continental replication of one common multispecies estimand
- FrogID validates the NAAMP all-stop effect
- temporal niche compression as a demonstrated result
- rainfall synchronizes frog communities
- conditional FrogID proves residual synchrony
- null residual diagnostics prove independence
- fixed-marginal shuffle controls all stop heterogeneity
- post-opening diagnostics were preregistered

## Remaining novelty risk

The paper sits close to established weather–frog activity literature and to general shared-environment/residual-association methods. Editorial success depends on foregrounding the **empirical decomposition and conditioning mismatch**, not the raw rain effect.
