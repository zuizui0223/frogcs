# Title audit — current endpoint paper

## Goal

The title should identify the empirical discovery without claiming:
- rainfall causality;
- synchrony;
- a universal mechanism;
- novelty for cross-site dependence itself;
- a named latent process.

## Current title

**Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation reveals recurrent within-taxon multi-site organization**

Strengths:
- accurate;
- includes both multi-site organization and recurrence;
- preserves observational wording.

Weaknesses:
- “reveals” and “organization” are abstract;
- “recurrent within-taxon multi-site organization” is dense and reads more like an internal synthesis term than natural ecological prose;
- it does not make the primary endpoint immediately legible.

## Candidate titles

### A — preferred
**Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation is concentrated within taxa across multiple sites**

Why:
- directly names the primary endpoint;
- avoids mechanism language;
- avoids making site recurrence co-equal with the primary endpoint;
- understandable without reading the null-model methods.

Trade-off:
- historical-site recurrence moves to the Abstract rather than the title.

### B
**Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation is concentrated within taxa and recurrent sites**

Why:
- includes both primary concentration and recurrence.

Trade-off:
- grammatical coordination can imply that the two forms of concentration are measured identically, which they are not.

### C
**Rainfall-associated frog chorus activity shows within-taxon concentration across recurrent sites**

Why:
- concise and empirical.

Trade-off:
- “across recurrent sites” may overstate the role of recurrent sites in the primary concentration statistic.

## Recommendation

Use **A** if retitling before submission. It best matches the current evidence hierarchy:

1. primary endpoint — within-taxon multi-site concentration;
2. biological interpretation — strong chorus state switching;
3. spatial interpretation — preferential expression at recurrent sites.

The current title remains defensible, so retitling is not required for scientific validity.
