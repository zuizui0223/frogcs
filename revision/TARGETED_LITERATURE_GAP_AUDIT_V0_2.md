# Targeted literature-gap audit v0.2

**Purpose:** test whether the integrated "fast environmental gate × persistent species × site chorus template" framing is genuinely distinct from established frog ecology literatures.

**Scope:** targeted gap audit, not a systematic review. It supports wording such as "previous studies have addressed these components separately"; it does **not** authorize "first ever" language.

## Literature-space diagnosis

The relevant literature has four well-developed edges but a missing centre:

```
weather cue → calling intensity/species response
            ↘
              [ MISSING: cue-gated re-expression
                of historical species × site structure ]
            ↗
site fidelity / persistent breeding-site use

fine-scale chorus interaction → within-chorus timing
community temporal dynamics → composition through time
```

frogcs occupies the missing centre by combining:

1. a short environmental contrast;
2. repeated fixed physical listening sites;
3. multiple frog species;
4. chorus intensity states, not only presence/absence;
5. historical species × site recurrence;
6. magnitude-matched nulls that ask where activation enters after total activation is held constant;
7. cross-fitting / strictly-prior history to separate transferable species response from local historical structure.

## Novelty hierarchy

### Too weak
"Rainfall increases frog calling."
Already established many times.

### Still weak
"Rainfall increases frog community richness."
Useful but unsurprising.

### Better
"Rainfall-associated community expansion is non-uniform across species × sites."
This is the RC11 boundary-allocation result.

### Strong integrated novelty
"Recent rain switches previously silent species × sites directly into strong chorus states, and the excess response deepens across multiple sites that have historically supported that species' strong chorusing."

### General ecological novelty
"A fast environmental gate can expose a slow spatial template of animal community activity."

The last claim is the conceptual contribution; the previous sentence is the frog-specific biological discovery that makes it credible.

## Why the result is surprising in frog biology

The intuitive rain-calling model predicts a graded response:
- more males call;
- more calls are detected;
- occasional new species appear.

The data instead show a conjunction of discrete and spatially organized changes:
- silent → CI2/CI3 transitions dominate activation;
- third-and-later occupied sites, not simply the first/second site, carry the unusual spatial excess;
- that spatial deepening is almost entirely strong chorus activity;
- historically strong physical sites preferentially regain strong chorus;
- species-specific rain sensitivity plus historical site propensity still cannot reproduce the full allocation.

The biological picture is therefore not "more croaking after rain" but **route-scale reassembly of strong reproductive acoustic states**.

## Frog-ecology wording boundaries

Use:
- reproductive acoustic behaviour;
- chorus-state transition;
- recurrent breeding/listening-site suitability;
- persistent chorus landscape;
- historically favoured species × site combinations;
- spatially coordinated strong chorusing.

Avoid unless independently demonstrated:
- individual memory;
- philopatry as the mechanism;
- migration into sites;
- colonization;
- occupancy gain;
- breeding success;
- spawning;
- causal rainfall trigger.

## Introduction gap paragraph — candidate text

Rainfall-sensitive calling is among the best-known features of anuran reproductive ecology, and climatic effects on calling have been quantified from individual species to whole assemblages. Separately, frog populations can exhibit strong breeding-site fidelity, while acoustic-community studies show rapid temporal turnover in which species are calling. These observations leave a different question unresolved: **when rain changes a frog assemblage over hours to days, is the wet-state community newly assembled from independent species responses, or does the environmental pulse re-express a persistent spatial organization of species and breeding sites?** Answering that question requires repeated physical sites, multiple species, an intensity measure capable of distinguishing marginal detection from full chorus, and a null that conditions on the total amount of activation while testing its spatial and taxonomic allocation.

## Discussion bridge to general ecology — candidate text

The frog system reveals a distinction that may apply broadly to behaviourally cryptic communities. A census made during an inactive state can hide spatial structure that has not disappeared ecologically; it is simply not expressed behaviourally. A short pulse can therefore change the observed community much faster than dispersal or demographic turnover by switching recurrent species × place combinations into an active state. We describe this as a **fast environmental gate acting on a slow spatial template**. In NAAMP frogs, that template is visible as repeated strong chorusing at particular physical sites, but the same logic could apply wherever animals alternate between cryptic and conspicuous states in response to episodic environmental cues.


## Addendum: higher-order spatial coherence after the prefixed test

A targeted follow-up search was conducted after the higher-order spatial-coherence endpoint was fixed. The relevant frog literature already contains **three kinds of spatial coherence**, all of which must be acknowledged.

1. **Within-chorus timing.** Neighboring males can overlap, alternate or synchronize calls within a pond or local chorus; this concerns communication timing among callers.
2. **Cross-site calling covariance.** Brooke et al. (2000) measured *Cophixalus ornatus* calling intensity at six locations along a 560-m transect and found substantial day-to-day variation shared among sites after accounting for persistent local differences.
3. **Regional population synchrony.** Trenham et al. (2003) found weak but detectable intraspecific synchrony of wetland-breeding frog/toad population fluctuations among sites separated by up to 50–100 km, while rainfall itself was more strongly synchronized.

Thus the novelty cannot be "frogs are spatially coherent" or "weather synchronizes calling". The unresolved level is the **allocation of a short behavioural pulse inside a repeated multi-species × multi-site matrix**.

Assemblage-wide acoustic studies sample many ponds and quantify temporal variation in calling composition or environmental drivers, but the focal response is typically site-level activity, composition or richness. The studies above likewise quantify covariance/synchrony among sites. They do not condition on both the number of newly recruited taxa and total extra-site spread and then ask whether residual spread is unusually concentrated within the same recruited taxa at third-and-later sites, nor link that higher-order concentration to species-specific historical physical sites.

The new prefixed higher-order test therefore sharpens the gap:

> **Does an environmental pulse produce only more site-level activations, or does it create higher-order route-scale coherence by concentrating extra spatial participation within the same newly activated taxa?**

Observed NAAMP higher-order within-species mass was β=1.524. At the observed route-new-species and extra-stop coefficients, the uniform-activation null predicted 1.138 and the persistence-preserving null 0.784; the conditional residual exceeded the upper 95% residual bound under both nulls (both P=0.000999).

This allows a stronger novelty formulation:

> **Previous frog studies establish environmental control of calling, rapid assemblage turnover, within-chorus call synchrony and breeding-site fidelity separately. Here, a repeated community matrix shows that rainfall-associated activation contains higher-order spatial dependence across sites: even at the same number of recruited taxa and the same total amount of spread, activity is unusually concentrated within the same taxa at third-and-later sites, and those strong chorus states preferentially reappear at historically used physical sites.**

The term **higher-order spatial coherence** is preferred over "synchrony" because NAAMP stops were surveyed sequentially rather than simultaneously.


## Addendum: breadth of the higher-order coherence result

The higher-order within-taxon coefficient is not concentrated in one taxon or one sampled state.

Taxonomic audit, using concentration thresholds reused from the earlier route-new spread audit:
- 53 taxa represented;
- 25 positive contributors;
- 19 contributors >=1% of positive mass;
- top-1 positive share 18.8%;
- top-5 54.2%;
- HHI 0.0855;
- every leave-one-taxon-out total coefficient positive.

Geographic audit, using the frozen RC11 leave-one-state-out rule:
- all 21 leave-one-state-out coefficients and 95% CIs positive;
- β range 0.967–1.775;
- minimum lower CI 0.397.

State-specific effects remain heterogeneous (14/17 positive point estimates, 2/17 positive CIs), so the evidence supports **broad but non-uniform** higher-order coherence within NAAMP, not spatial invariance.


## Closest-prior-study correction

Two studies materially narrow the novelty claim and are now explicitly incorporated into the manuscript.

- Brooke, Alford & Schwarzkopf (2000), *Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology* 49:79–87, DOI 10.1007/s002650000256: one species, six locations along a 560-m transect, environmental/social controls of calling and shared day-to-day variation among sites.
- Trenham et al. (2003), *Ecological Applications* 13:1522–1532, DOI 10.1890/02-5206: eight wetland-breeding species, regional population turnover and weak spatial synchrony up to 50–100 km, with rainfall correlations on 1–4-year lags.

Accordingly, avoid:
- "first evidence of spatial coherence in frog choruses";
- "first evidence that rainfall coordinates frog activity across sites";
- "frog sites respond independently in prior work".

Preferred novelty statement:

> **This study resolves a different order of structure: after conditioning on how many taxa are recruited and how much total extra-site spread occurs, rainfall-associated activity is still disproportionately concentrated within the same recruited taxa at third-and-later sites; this higher-order concentration survives cross-fitted species response, prior site history, observer/site controls, and is distributed across taxa and states.**

That distinction is substantially narrower and more defensible than a generic synchrony claim.

## 2026 novelty lock: what the closest adjacent literatures already do

A targeted 2026 re-audit sharpened the boundary around the phrase **pulse-revealed dependence structure**.

### Pulse ecology

Yang et al. (2008) and subsequent pulse-dynamics theory explicitly treat pulses as drivers of behaviour, population/community responses, persistent effects and spatial patch dynamics. Jentsch et al. (2019) further formalize patch dynamics as one of four core dimensions of pulse responses.

**Therefore do not claim:**
- that ecology has ignored spatial structure during pulses;
- that pulses have never been linked to persistent community effects;
- that this is the first study to connect pulse dynamics with spatial heterogeneity.

**What remains distinct here:** the response variable is the **dependence structure of a repeated species × place activity matrix**, tested after the first-order magnitude of activation, taxon-specific response and historical site propensity are already represented.

### Spatial latent-structure modelling

Spatial joint species distribution models explicitly recover "hidden spatial structure" through latent spatial factors (e.g. Ovaskainen et al. 2016).

**Therefore avoid headline language such as:**
- "first discovery of hidden spatial community structure";
- "reveals latent spatial structure" without qualification.

That vocabulary already has a strong statistical meaning in community modelling.

**Preferred wording:** **pulse-revealed dependence structure** or **conditional within-taxon multi-site organization**.

### Frog chorusing and multi-site weather response

Brooke et al. (2000) showed that calling activity covaries across multiple locations while substantial site-specific variation remains. Recent passive-acoustic work (Rush et al. 2026) shows strong geographic/site variation in frog calling phenology and contrasting rainfall relationships among regions. Other multi-site acoustic studies likewise model site-level calling as a function of precipitation, hydroperiod and local habitat.

These papers establish:
- weather-linked calling;
- cross-site covariance;
- persistent site differences;
- region-specific environmental response windows.

They do **not** ask whether the allocation of newly expressed activity across repeated fixed sites remains unusually concentrated **within the same taxa after taxon-specific rainfall sensitivity and prior species × site use are modelled**.

### Novelty statement authorized after the re-audit

The strongest defensible statement is:

> **The study tests a dependence-level response to an environmental pulse: whether multi-site activation remains concentrated within recruited taxa after transferable taxon responses, prior physical-site use, dry-state persistence and total activation magnitude are represented.**

The biological extension is:

> **Strong chorus states preferentially reappear at the same taxon-specific physical sites, so the short-term pulse is associated with re-expression of a recurrent species × place chorus template.**

The general ecological extension is:

> **Environmental pulses may expose dependence in joint species × place activity that is not recoverable from marginal species responses or static site propensities alone.**

### Claims not authorized

Do not write:
- "first demonstration that pulses reveal hidden structure";
- "first evidence of spatial memory";
- "first demonstration of cross-site coherence in frogs";
- "rain synchronizes a latent network";
- "higher-order community interaction";
- "universal pulse-revealed template".

The novelty is narrower, testable and stronger: **conditional dependence after explicit first-order ecological comparators**.
