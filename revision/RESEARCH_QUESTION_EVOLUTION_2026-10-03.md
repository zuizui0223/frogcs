# Research-question evolution and discovery history — 2026-10-03

## Why this document exists

The current manuscript should not be narrated as if the final structural interpretation was the original hypothesis.

It was not.

The repository developed through repeated falsification, reframing and abandonment of earlier stories. The present question — what spatial unit organizes frog reproductive acoustic activity on favourable nights — emerged only after several simpler interpretations failed.

This document records that history so that the paper, cover letter and future talks do not retrospectively make the project look more prespecified or conceptually tidy than it was.

---

## 1. Starting point: rainfall-associated chorus synchrony / co-calling

### Initial question

The earliest frogcs line asked approximately:

> **Does recent rainfall make multiple frog species call together over short time windows, compressing temporal niches or synchronizing the acoustic community?**

The early package was explicitly framed around multispecies co-calling and synchrony, with NAAMP and FrogID used as complementary acoustic systems.

### Why it was attractive

This was a natural ecological question:
- rainfall is a familiar cue for frog reproductive activity;
- short environmental pulses could plausibly synchronize multiple species;
- synchrony has consequences for acoustic competition, mating opportunity and community organization.

### What broke

When activity amount was separated from association structure, the synchrony interpretation weakened.

The data were much more consistent with:
- more species becoming acoustically active;
- more sites becoming active;
- species differing in their rainfall responses;

than with a new pairwise or network-level association among species.

### Consequence

The project moved away from:
- temporal-niche compression;
- rain-induced multispecies synchrony;
- co-calling interaction as the main phenomenon.

This was the first major conceptual reset.

---

## 2. First replacement story: species-selective community reassembly

### New question

Once synchrony weakened, the focus shifted to:

> **Does rainfall selectively recruit some frog species more strongly than others, changing acoustic-community composition rather than merely total activity?**

This produced the ecological-pulse, species-specific rainfall-response and within-family heterogeneity analyses.

### Why it was plausible

If rainfall acts as an environmental filter, one might expect:
- explosive versus prolonged breeders to differ;
- body size, life history or breeding ecology to predict responses;
- the wet-state assemblage to be a non-random subset of the regional pool.

### What was found

Species responses were heterogeneous.

But the proposed low-dimensional explanations were weak:
- body-size interpretations did not survive validation cleanly;
- functional/life-history trait explanations were not compelling;
- within-family selectivity existed, but did not by itself generate a strong new ecological principle.

### Consequence

“Species-selective reassembly” remained descriptively useful, but was too close to already-known species-specific calling responses to carry the paper.

---

## 3. Timescale and context exploration: how long does the pulse last, and what modifies it?

The repository then tested several contextual stories:
- rainfall-pulse timescale;
- hydroperiod filters;
- breeding-season filters;
- episodic-breeder context;
- historical route dryness;
- response diversity;
- community-memory style models.

### Why these were tried

At this stage the project was still trying to explain **which species respond and under what conditions**.

### What happened

Several candidate mechanisms were unsupported, unstable or non-estimable.

The important negative lesson was:

> no simple trait, seasonal-context or compositional-memory explanation was strong enough to organize the manuscript.

This is one place where the project began to look “AI-like”: many plausible explanations were generated and tested in rapid sequence.

The appropriate historical interpretation is not that these were a single coherent hypothesis family. They were exploratory attempts to explain a heterogeneous rainfall-associated acoustic response.

---

## 4. Spatial turn: from “which species respond?” to “how is response distributed across sites?”

The next major change occurred when the analyses stopped treating route-level richness or composition as the only response and began using the ten fixed NAAMP stops explicitly.

### Emerging question

> **When a taxon becomes acoustically active after recent rain, does it appear at one stop, or across several separated stops?**

This led to:
- species × stop incidence decomposition;
- second-stop, third-and-later and fourth-and-later participation;
- uniform-activation and persistence-preserving nulls;
- route-topology diagnostics.

### Initial temptation

There was briefly a “third-stop” interpretation.

### What the marginal-depth audit showed

That interpretation was wrong.

The second and third marginal stops were not exceptional.

The excess was a **deep tail**:
- marginal depths 1–3 were consistent with activation nulls;
- marginal depths 4–10 exceeded them.

### Consequence

The biological object changed again.

The paper was no longer about a threshold such as “three sites”.
It became about:

> **unusually deep multi-site expression of the same recruited taxon.**

This was the first direct ancestor of the current manuscript.

---

## 5. Activation geometry: an attractive explanation that failed its specificity test

A species-specific “activation geometry” appeared repeatable across time and route splits.

### Why it looked important

It seemed possible that species had stable rainfall-response spatial strategies:
- some species might expand toward route edges;
- others might deepen locally;
- those spatial response types might constitute ecological traits.

### Falsification

The same geometry was highly repeatable under:
- reverse-dry comparisons;
- low-rainfall-contrast comparisons;
- baseline spatial structure.

Therefore the repeatability was not rainfall-specific.

### Consequence

Activation geometry was demoted to SI.

This was a crucial methodological lesson:

> repeatability of a spatial pattern does not show that the pattern is a rainfall-response mechanism.

The project then shifted from “species have rainfall-specific geometries” to a more defensible question about **residual allocation after first-order species and site structure are represented**.

---

## 6. Within-taxon concentration: the current principal result appears

The next step asked a sharper conditional question.

For route-new acoustic taxa, suppose we already know:
- how many taxa become active;
- how many total extra-stop incidences occur.

Then ask:

> **Are those extra incidences distributed across taxa as expected, or are they disproportionately concentrated within the same taxa?**

This produced the within-taxon concentration endpoint.

### Why this mattered

A route can gain the same amount of calling in different ways.

Example:

- four taxa each appearing at one extra stop;
- one taxon appearing at four extra stops.

The total amount of new calling can be identical, but the spatial organization is different.

### Stronger comparator

The eventual principal comparator allowed:
- route-cross-fitted species-specific rainfall response;
- strictly-prior species × physical-SiteID propensity;
- dry-state persistence;
- pair-level matching of total wet incidence.

Even then, observed concentration remained higher than predicted.

This became the empirical core of the current paper.

---

## 7. Historical physical sites: the second piece of the present story

The next question was:

> **If strong activity recurs, does it recur at the same physical places?**

Within pair × taxon comparisons showed that physical SiteIDs with prior CI2/3 were more likely to host later wet-state CI2/3.

A directional wet-versus-dry targeting analysis further showed stronger full-chorus targeting toward the rain-favoured survey.

### What this did — and did not — establish

It established recurrent taxon-specific chorus locations.

It did **not** establish:
- individual memory;
- philopatry;
- movement back to the same site;
- breeding success at those sites.

At this stage the manuscript contained two facts:

1. wet-state activity was unusually concentrated within taxa across sites;
2. strong calling recurred at taxon-specific physical sites.

But the causal or structural relation between those facts was still unclear.

---

## 8. The user’s “so what?” objection forced the key conceptual step

The important challenge was:

> **If the same species is concentrated across multiple sites, what does that actually tell us about frog ecology?**

This exposed a weakness in the RC4-era story.

“Residual dependence exists” is statistically valid but biologically incomplete.

Three broad explanations were considered:

1. measured common environmental forcing across a route;
2. a species-level route-night breeding/calling state;
3. local social or spatial propagation among sites.

This was the explicit reopening of same-data mechanism analysis on 2026-10-03.

---

## 9. Common-cause falsification: measured weather is insufficient

The reopened chain first asked whether the residual dependence was simply omitted weather structure.

Tests added:
- nonlinear rain recency;
- nonlinear temperature;
- seasonal harmonics;
- interactions;
- actual 72-h ERA5 precipitation;
- measured hearing/noise/wind conditions;
- species-level calibration;
- leave-one-taxon-out stop-night “hotness” from other taxa.

### Result

These additions explained little of the within-taxon concentration residual.

This did **not** rule out all shared environmental forcing.

It ruled out the specific claim that the result is easily reducible to the measured common environmental and observational variables available in these data.

---

## 10. Spatial scale of dependence: neither independent ponds nor a uniform route switch

Bounded residual dependence was then estimated within species × route-night.

For dry-route-silent taxa:
- near route-position lags 1–3 remained strongly dependent;
- far lags 7–9 retained most of that dependence.

A route-cluster bootstrap nevertheless detected a modest near-to-far decline.

### Interpretation

This constrained the spatial organization.

The pattern was inconsistent with:
- fully independent stop responses;

but also not spatially flat.

A cross-fitted scalar species-night random-intercept generator made all ten stops too coherent.

### Consequence

The natural ecological question became:

> **Is frog reproductive acoustic activity organized at an intermediate spatial scale — broader than an individual wetland but more selective than a whole-route switch?**

This is the clearest present-day formulation of the problem.

---

## 11. Exact-k historical-template alignment: linking deep response to recurrent places

The final post-hoc structural step asked whether the non-uniform response was arbitrary.

For every eligible dry-route-silent taxon × night:
- observed wet depth k was fixed exactly;
- the strongest fixed-q site propensities were retained;
- only **which k of ten sites were active** was randomized under the conditional independent-Bernoulli distribution.

The question was:

> **Given that this taxon was active at k sites tonight, were those sites unusually likely to be the physical sites where it had chorused strongly before?**

### Result

Among deep k≥4 responses, historical strong-chorus SiteIDs were used more than the fixed-q expectation.

The direct deep-versus-shallow contrast was also positive.

### Why this is different from ordinary site fidelity

The test does not simply show that “good sites are good again”.

It asks whether **the spatial placement of an already-deep event** aligns with historical strong sites after:
- depth k;
- current modeled site propensity;
- route-night conditions represented in q

are held constant.

This provided the direct bridge between:
- route-spanning species-night dependence;
- recurrent species-specific physical locations.

---

## 12. Current ecological interpretation

The repository should now avoid presenting “fast gate × slow template” as if it were the original hypothesis or a named mechanism.

A more natural statement is:

> **On favourable nights, frog reproductive acoustic activity appears to be organized at an intermediate spatial scale. Stops do not behave as fully independent local responses, but taxa also do not switch uniformly across whole routes. Instead, route-spanning activity is spatially selective and disproportionately involves recurrent taxon-specific chorus locations.**

A shorter natural-history question is:

> **When a frog species becomes active after rain, does it call independently at individual wetlands, everywhere across the local landscape, or preferentially across a recurrent subset of places?**

The NAAMP evidence favours the third description.

---

## 13. What is actually novel versus what was already known

Already known:
- rainfall-sensitive frog calling;
- species-specific meteorological responses;
- cross-site chorus covariance;
- breeding/calling-site fidelity;
- persistent differences among sites.

The current contribution is narrower:

> **conditional spatial organization of a pulse: after response magnitude, species rainfall response and historical site propensity are represented, activity remains non-independent across sites, and unusually deep expression is selectively associated with recurrent strong-chorus locations.**

This is why Brooke et al. (2000) is a particularly important precedent rather than a threat to hide:
it already contains both shared temporal variation and place reuse.

The new question is how those two ingredients are coupled in the realized multi-site response.

---

## 14. Why the project can look AI-generated

The repository contains rapid sequences of:
- freeze;
- implement;
- run;
- falsify;
- redefine;
- freeze again.

That workflow is auditable, but it can create a false narrative if the final concept is projected backward onto the beginning.

Specific risks:
- presenting a discovered endpoint as an original hypothesis;
- using polished labels such as “fast gate” and “slow template” as if they preceded the data;
- treating every null-model diagnostic as a biological question;
- allowing the number of post-hoc tests to substitute for independent evidence.

The correct remedy is **not to hide the exploration**.

The correct remedy is:
- keep the main biological questions simple;
- distinguish discovery from defence/falsification analyses;
- label the mechanistic refinement post-hoc;
- stop same-data mechanism fishing;
- require an external prospectively frozen test for evidential upgrading.

## 16. Writing rule for the manuscript

Main-text questions should remain three natural-history questions:

1. **State change:** does recent-rain activity amplify existing calling or switch silent cells into substantial chorus?
2. **Spatial unit:** when a taxon becomes active, are route stops independent, or is activity organized across separated locations?
3. **Place selection:** when activity becomes spatially deep, is it uniform across the route or preferentially expressed at recurrent taxon-specific chorus locations?

Everything else — weather nulls, calibration, stop-night hotness, bounded residual coefficients, scalar latent-state generators, Monte Carlo stability and monitoring covariance — is evidence used to **defend or refine those questions**, not a separate list of biological questions.
