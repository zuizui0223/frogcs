# Higher-order chorus coherence synthesis v0.2

## Central result

Across repeated NAAMP routes, recent-rain conditions are associated with a community-level transition in frog reproductive acoustic activity:

> **silent species × site cells often enter strong chorus states, newly recruited taxa deepen disproportionately across multiple sites, and that higher-order multi-site expression preferentially returns to historically strong species-specific physical sites.**

The novel unit of response is therefore not richness alone, nor cross-site covariance alone, but **within-taxon spatial dependence after first-order recruitment and spread are conditioned away**.

## General ecological principle

> **Fast environmental gates can expose higher-order spatial organization on slow species × place templates.**

The conceptual shift is from:

> how much does a pulse increase activity?

to:

> **what dependence structure becomes expressed when activity switches on?**

## Frog ecology contribution

The study supports three frog-specific conclusions:

1. **Wet-state change often resembles chorus-state switching**, including direct silence→full-chorus transitions.
2. **The spatial response is deeper than isolated recruitment**, with the unusual component appearing at third-and-later sites and dominated by strong chorus.
3. **Strong multi-site expression is historically placed**, recurring preferentially at species-specific sites that previously supported strong choruses.

## What the study does not solve

The lower-level generator remains unresolved. Plausible contributors include:
- fine-scale hydrology;
- vegetation/microtopography;
- breeding phenology;
- local density;
- individual/site fidelity;
- social processes;
- interactions among these.

Existing coarse proxies were insufficient but do not eliminate those mechanisms.
