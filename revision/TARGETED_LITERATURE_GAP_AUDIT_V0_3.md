# Targeted literature-gap audit v0.3 — closest prior studies

**Purpose:** reassess whether the current RC4 claim remains novel after explicitly comparing it with the closest frog-chorus, anuran-metacommunity and environmental-pulse studies available through October 2026.

**Scope:** targeted gap audit, not a systematic review. It does not authorize "first ever" language.

## Closest studies, ordered by biological similarity

### 1. Sarker et al. 2022 — closest pulse × multispecies × multisite analogue

Sarker et al. (*Ecological Indicators* 145:109640; DOI 10.1016/j.ecolind.2022.109640) monitored 16 wetlands over five years and deployed acoustic recorders at six sites. For two environmental-water releases, they recorded four nights immediately before and after arrival of river flow. Nine frog species were detected acoustically; richness increased from six to eight species across the before/after windows, but responses varied among species and sites.

Why it is close:
- short hydrological pulse;
- multiple frog species;
- multiple physical sites;
- calling/chorus responses;
- before/after environmental contrast.

What it does not test:
- repeated ten-stop species × site matrices;
- how a fixed total amount of acoustic activation is allocated among taxa;
- residual within-taxon site-pair concentration;
- taxon-specific rainfall response + strictly-prior site history + dry-state persistence in one null;
- historical re-expression at the same physical SiteIDs.

**Novelty implication:** frogcs must not claim that it is the first study of a hydrological pulse reorganizing frog chorusing across multiple sites. Its novelty begins after that point: the **conditional allocation/dependence structure** of the pulse.

### 2. Brodie et al. 2025 — closest rain-triggered multispecies chorus study

Brodie et al. (*Ecosphere* 16:e70153; DOI 10.1002/ecs2.70153) monitored a tropical-savanna anuran community at three breeding sites over two wet seasons. Rain events often initiated breeding, and explosive-breeding species chorused on the night of, or immediately after, the first major rainfall event. Species differed strongly in their environmental responses.

Why it is close:
- rainfall-triggered chorus activation;
- multispecies community;
- repeated breeding sites;
- chorus phenology/intensity.

What it does not test:
- route-scale multi-site depth of the same taxon;
- conditioning on total activation magnitude;
- residual concentration of calling incidences within taxa;
- historical same-physical-site re-expression across many years.

**Novelty implication:** "rain switches choruses on" and species-specific rain response are not novel. Those effects belong in the comparator/background, not the headline.

### 3. Ceron et al. 2020 — closest anuran metacommunity climate/synchrony study

Ceron et al. (*Ecology and Evolution* 10:4630–4639; DOI 10.1002/ece3.6217) recorded 25 species at six sites across one year. They explicitly tested spatial synchrony in calling activity and how temperature, rainfall and humidity shaped species composition through time. Calling dynamics were weakly synchronous and climate relationships were nonstationary.

Why it is close:
- multiple species × multiple sites;
- calling activity as community state;
- climate;
- explicit synchrony/metacommunity analysis.

What it does not test:
- short matched environmental pulses;
- repeated species × physical-site history across years;
- dependence remaining after species-specific response and total activity are represented.

**Novelty implication:** frogcs cannot sell "spatially coherent calling" or "weather structures anuran metacommunities" as new.

### 4. Brooke et al. 2000 — closest fine-scale cross-site calling covariance study

Brooke, Alford & Schwarzkopf (*Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology* 49:79–87; DOI 10.1007/s002650000256) measured one species, *Cophixalus ornatus*, at six locations along a 560-m transect. Calling varied among sites and days, with up to 35.8% of day-to-day variation attributable to factors common across sites after persistent site differences were removed.

Why it is close:
- spatially separated calling locations;
- common environmental/social forcing;
- persistent local differences plus shared temporal variation.

What it does not test:
- multispecies community allocation;
- within-taxon concentration conditional on total spread;
- recurrent species × SiteID templates over many years.

**Novelty implication:** cross-site common forcing is known; the new object must be described as **conditional allocation**, not generic coherence.

### 5. Rush et al. 2026 — closest recent multi-site climatic calling study

Rush et al. (*Ecology and Evolution* 16:e74191; DOI 10.1002/ece3.74191) processed near-continuous recordings from ten populations of *Pseudophryne covacevichae* over one year, detecting approximately 350,000 advertisement calls. Calling phenology differed markedly among sites/regions, and antecedent rainfall effects differed in sign and timescale between regions.

Why it is close:
- ten sites;
- dense temporal acoustic coverage;
- antecedent rainfall windows;
- spatial heterogeneity in environmental response.

What it does not test:
- multispecies allocation;
- repeated historical site use across years;
- within-taxon site-pair concentration after first-order response is controlled.

**Novelty implication:** site-specific rain windows and heterogeneous response are already established.

### 6. Trenham et al. 2003 — closest broad spatial synchrony/turnover study

Trenham et al. (*Ecological Applications* 13:1522–1532; DOI 10.1890/02-5206) used a statewide frog monitoring network for eight wetland-breeding species. Population fluctuations were weakly synchronized among sites separated by up to 50–100 km, and abundance correlated with rainfall 1–4 years earlier.

Why it is close:
- broad monitoring network;
- multiple species;
- spatial synchrony and turnover;
- rainfall.

What it does not test:
- short rainfall-recency contrasts;
- chorus-state intensity;
- allocation of simultaneous survey-period calling among repeated route stops.

**Novelty implication:** regional synchrony is not the novelty.

### 7. Sugai et al. 2021 — closest assemblage-wide fine-temporal calling study

Sugai et al. (*Journal of Animal Ecology* 90:673–684; DOI 10.1111/1365-2656.13399) investigated 39 anuran assemblages in the Pantanal and showed that calling composition changed strongly with temporal resolution and local/landscape context.

Why it is close:
- assemblage-wide acoustic ecology;
- fine temporal dynamics;
- beta/compositional structure.

What it does not test:
- matched rainfall pulses;
- repeated fixed species × site histories;
- residual within-taxon site-pair concentration.

## What is already established and therefore not novel

Do not sell any of the following:

1. rain or humidity affects frog calling;
2. rain can initiate explosive breeding choruses;
3. chorus richness/composition can change rapidly;
4. responses differ among species;
5. responses differ among sites or regions;
6. calling can covary across separated locations;
7. frog populations can show site fidelity;
8. environmental pulses can alter community state;
9. species × site incidence matrices can be analysed with constrained null models.

## What remains distinct in frogcs

The present study asks a different conditional question.

For each wetter–drier matched route pair:
- taxa with no calling record anywhere on the drier route can become acoustically expressed at multiple wetter-survey stops;
- the total number of such taxa and the total amount of multi-site calling are first-order quantities;
- the focal quantity asks whether the same total spread is **more concentrated within the same taxa** than expected.

The principal comparator then already contains:
1. route-cross-fitted taxon-specific rainfall response;
2. strictly-prior taxon × physical-SiteID calling propensity;
3. dry-state persistence;
4. pair-level matching of total wet calling incidence.

Even under this comparator, observed within-taxon concentration exceeds prediction.

The strongest empirical novelty is therefore:

> **Recent-rain calling is allocated across a repeated species × place matrix in a way that remains more concentrated within taxa than expected after transferable taxon response, prior physical-site use, dry-state persistence and total activity magnitude are represented.**

The biological extension is:

> **The deep calling-incidence tail is expressed at separated route stops, and strong chorusing is preferentially re-expressed at taxon-specific physical sites that had supported strong chorusing previously.**

## Why the exact-depth audit improves the novelty claim

The current result should no longer be sold as a "third-stop threshold".

The post-freeze exact-depth audit shows:
- marginal calling-incidence depths 1–3 fall within both primary activation-null envelopes;
- marginal depths 4–10 exceed both;
- observed marginal coefficients decline with depth;
- null expectations decline much faster.

Thus the spatial signal is:

> **a heavier/deeper within-taxon calling-incidence tail than expected**

not:
- exponential growth;
- a threshold at the third stop;
- movement or arrival across successive sites.

This makes the claim more interpretable and less method-dependent.

## Novelty hierarchy after the closest-study audit

### Not novel
> Rain activates frog choruses.

### Not novel
> Environmental pulses alter frog richness and chorus composition across sites.

### Not novel
> Frog calling can covary across sites and retain persistent site differences.

### Moderately novel
> Recent-rain calling produces a deeper-than-expected multi-site incidence tail within the same taxa across a large repeated monitoring network.

### Strongest defensible novelty
> **After the amount of activation, transferable taxon-specific rain response and historical physical-site propensity are already represented, the allocation of calling activity across sites remains conditionally dependent within taxa.**

### General ecological contribution
> **Environmental pulses may expose dependence in joint species × place activity that is not recoverable from marginal responses and static site propensities alone.**

This is a hypothesis-generating generalization from one frog monitoring system, not a demonstrated universal law.

## Search conclusion

The targeted literature search through October 2026 found studies that separately or jointly cover:
- short hydrological pulses and multispecies chorusing;
- rainfall-triggered chorus onset;
- multisite calling covariance;
- metacommunity synchrony;
- broad monitoring-network turnover;
- site fidelity;
- fine-temporal assemblage dynamics.

No study located in this audit combined all of the following in one inferential test:
- repeated fixed multi-species × multi-site matrices;
- a short rain-associated behavioural contrast;
- chorus-state intensity;
- conditioning on total acoustic activation;
- taxon-specific environmental response;
- strictly-prior taxon × physical-site history;
- residual within-taxon site-pair concentration;
- historical re-expression at the same physical sites.

Accordingly, use **"to our knowledge"** only if needed and avoid categorical "first" language. The novelty is best defended as a new **conditional estimand and ecological question**, not as the first observation of any one component phenomenon.
