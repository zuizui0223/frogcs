# Targeted literature-gap audit v0.1

**Purpose:** test whether the integrated "fast environmental gate × persistent species × site chorus template" framing is genuinely distinct from established frog ecology literatures.

**Scope:** targeted gap audit, not a systematic review. It supports wording such as "previous studies have addressed these components separately"; it does **not** authorize "first ever" language.

## Literature-space diagnosis

The relevant literature has four well-developed edges but a missing centre:

```
weather cue → calling intensity/species response
            ↘
              [ MISSING: cue-gated re-expression
                of historical species × site structure ]
            ↗
site fidelity / persistent breeding-site use

fine-scale chorus interaction → within-chorus timing
community temporal dynamics → composition through time
```

frogcs occupies the missing centre by combining:

1. a short environmental contrast;
2. repeated fixed physical listening sites;
3. multiple frog species;
4. chorus intensity states, not only presence/absence;
5. historical species × site recurrence;
6. magnitude-matched nulls that ask where activation enters after total activation is held constant;
7. cross-fitting / strictly-prior history to separate transferable species response from local historical structure.

## Novelty hierarchy

### Too weak
"Rainfall increases frog calling."
Already established many times.

### Still weak
"Rainfall increases frog community richness."
Useful but unsurprising.

### Better
"Rainfall-associated community expansion is non-uniform across species × sites."
This is the RC11 boundary-allocation result.

### Strong integrated novelty
"Recent rain switches previously silent species × sites directly into strong chorus states, and the excess response deepens across multiple sites that have historically supported that species' strong chorusing."

### General ecological novelty
"A fast environmental gate can expose a slow spatial template of animal community activity."

The last claim is the conceptual contribution; the previous sentence is the frog-specific biological discovery that makes it credible.

## Why the result is surprising in frog biology

The intuitive rain-calling model predicts a graded response:
- more males call;
- more calls are detected;
- occasional new species appear.

The data instead show a conjunction of discrete and spatially organized changes:
- silent → CI2/CI3 transitions dominate activation;
- third-and-later occupied sites, not simply the first/second site, carry the unusual spatial excess;
- that spatial deepening is almost entirely strong chorus activity;
- historically strong physical sites preferentially regain strong chorus;
- species-specific rain sensitivity plus historical site propensity still cannot reproduce the full allocation.

The biological picture is therefore not "more croaking after rain" but **route-scale reassembly of strong reproductive acoustic states**.

## Frog-ecology wording boundaries

Use:
- reproductive acoustic behaviour;
- chorus-state transition;
- recurrent breeding/listening-site suitability;
- persistent chorus landscape;
- historically favoured species × site combinations;
- spatially coordinated strong chorusing.

Avoid unless independently demonstrated:
- individual memory;
- philopatry as the mechanism;
- migration into sites;
- colonization;
- occupancy gain;
- breeding success;
- spawning;
- causal rainfall trigger.

## Introduction gap paragraph — candidate text

Rainfall-sensitive calling is among the best-known features of anuran reproductive ecology, and climatic effects on calling have been quantified from individual species to whole assemblages. Separately, frog populations can exhibit strong breeding-site fidelity, while acoustic-community studies show rapid temporal turnover in which species are calling. These observations leave a different question unresolved: **when rain changes a frog assemblage over hours to days, is the wet-state community newly assembled from independent species responses, or does the environmental pulse re-express a persistent spatial organization of species and breeding sites?** Answering that question requires repeated physical sites, multiple species, an intensity measure capable of distinguishing marginal detection from full chorus, and a null that conditions on the total amount of activation while testing its spatial and taxonomic allocation.

## Discussion bridge to general ecology — candidate text

The frog system reveals a distinction that may apply broadly to behaviourally cryptic communities. A census made during an inactive state can hide spatial structure that has not disappeared ecologically; it is simply not expressed behaviourally. A short pulse can therefore change the observed community much faster than dispersal or demographic turnover by switching recurrent species × place combinations into an active state. We describe this as a **fast environmental gate acting on a slow spatial template**. In NAAMP frogs, that template is visible as repeated strong chorusing at particular physical sites, but the same logic could apply wherever animals alternate between cryptic and conspicuous states in response to episodic environmental cues.


## Addendum: higher-order spatial coherence after the prefixed test

A targeted follow-up search was conducted after the higher-order spatial-coherence endpoint was fixed. The closest anuran "synchrony" literature mainly concerns **within-chorus call timing among individual males**: neighboring males may overlap, alternate or synchronize calls within a pond or local chorus. That literature addresses communication timing and receiver consequences, not how rainfall-associated recruitment is distributed across repeated physical breeding/listening sites at the community scale.

Assemblage-wide acoustic studies sample many ponds and quantify temporal variation in calling composition or environmental drivers, but the focal response is typically site-level activity, composition or richness. They do not condition on both the number of newly recruited taxa and total extra-site spread and then test whether residual spread is unusually concentrated within the same taxa at third-and-later sites.

The new prefixed higher-order test therefore sharpens the gap:

> **Does an environmental pulse produce only more site-level activations, or does it create higher-order route-scale coherence by concentrating extra spatial participation within the same newly activated taxa?**

Observed NAAMP higher-order within-species mass was β=1.524. At the observed route-new-species and extra-stop coefficients, the uniform-activation null predicted 1.138 and the persistence-preserving null 0.784; the conditional residual exceeded the upper 95% residual bound under both nulls (both P=0.000999).

This allows a stronger novelty formulation:

> **Previous frog studies establish environmental control of calling, rapid assemblage turnover, within-chorus call synchrony and breeding-site fidelity separately. Here, a repeated community matrix shows that rainfall-associated activation contains higher-order spatial dependence across sites: even at the same number of recruited taxa and the same total amount of spread, activity is unusually concentrated within the same taxa at third-and-later sites, and those strong chorus states preferentially reappear at historically used physical sites.**

The term **higher-order spatial coherence** is preferred over "synchrony" because NAAMP stops were surveyed sequentially rather than simultaneously.
