# Novelty and general-principle audit v0.2

## Bottom line

The paper supports a genuinely distinct ecological finding, but **not** the generic claim that joint ecological responses contain information beyond marginal responses.

That distinction is already central to:
- spatial synchrony theory, where shared environmental forcing can induce dependence among locations;
- joint species-distribution models, which separate environmental responses from residual association;
- environment-dependent JSDMs, which allow residual associations themselves to vary with environmental context.

Therefore the manuscript must not present “marginal versus joint” as a new statistical principle.

## What is already known

The literature already supports:
1. rainfall/weather can increase frog calling;
2. anuran responses differ among taxa;
3. frog chorusing can covary across sites;
4. persistent species × wetland calling structure exists;
5. environmental pulses can alter animal behaviour and community state;
6. common environmental forcing can synchronize spatially separated populations;
7. residual joint association can remain after modeled environmental responses.

None of those is the central novelty claim.

## What the NAAMP result adds

The distinct biological problem is the **allocation of a short behavioural activation pulse across a repeated spatial template**.

The primary comparator is allowed to know:
- transferable taxon-specific rainfall response;
- strictly prior taxon × physical-site propensity;
- dry-state persistence;
- total expected wet-state incidence.

Yet observed wet-state activation remains over-concentrated within taxa:
- observed concentration: **1.650**;
- principal prediction: **1.353**;
- residual: **0.297**;
- null 95% range: **−0.132 to 0.119**;
- P = **0.000999**.

The excess is biologically structured:
- marginal depths 4–10 are overrepresented;
- deep spread is dominated by CI2/3;
- prior strong-chorus sites predict strong wet-state chorusing;
- exact-k deep placement preferentially overlaps prior-strong sites;
- a uniform whole-route species-night switch is too coherent.

Thus the new empirical object is not “dependence” alone. It is a **history-dependent response allocation of pulse-triggered behavioural activation within taxa across sites**.

## General organizing hypothesis

For a short environmental pulse, distinguish:

### 1. Marginal activation
Which local taxon × site units become active, and how much total activity occurs.

### 2. Conditional allocation
Given that first-order activation, how the realized active cells are distributed among taxa and spatial locations.

Existing pulse theory already incorporates the spatial distribution of pulse events and resource patches. The broader ecological hypothesis here instead concerns the response after that forcing:

> **Two landscapes with the same amount of local activation can realize different behavioural states because activity can be allocated differently across taxa and persistent places.**

This is an ecological hypothesis about the spatial allocation of pulse-triggered behaviour after first-order response is represented, not a mathematical theorem about joint distributions or a claim that spatial pulse structure is new.

## Why the frog result is useful

In the NAAMP system, the conditional-allocation component has three properties:
1. **taxon concentration** — activation is more deeply bundled within taxa than predicted;
2. **spatial selectivity** — the response is not a uniform route-wide switch;
3. **historical anchoring** — deep activity disproportionately uses recurrent taxon-specific strong-chorus locations.

This combination is what gives the result ecological content beyond generic residual covariance.

## Strongest defensible novelty sentence

> **After first-order taxon response, prior physical-site propensity, dry-state persistence and total activation were represented, rain-associated frog calling remained over-concentrated within taxa across separated sites, with the deepest expression preferentially involving recurrent taxon-specific chorus locations.**

## Strongest defensible generality sentence

> **Pulse-triggered behavioural responses may have an allocation component: the same amount of local activation can generate different landscape-level states depending on how activity is bundled among taxa and persistent places.**

## Claims to avoid

Do not claim:
- first discovery of spatial synchrony;
- first evidence of residual ecological association;
- first proof that joint distributions differ from marginal distributions;
- first evidence that environment changes species associations;
- first evidence of recurrent frog calling sites;
- a universal pulse-allocation law;
- an identified causal mechanism.

## Evidential status

The result is broad within NAAMP but manuscript-level exploratory because the endpoint was developed after opening the dataset.

Prospective external replication can test transferability of the frozen allocation endpoint. It is an evidential upgrade, not a prerequisite for the current NAAMP paper.
