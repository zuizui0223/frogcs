# Integrated evidence hierarchy and claim discipline v0.1

## Central biological claim

**Recent-rain conditions are associated with higher-order re-expression of a persistent species × site chorus structure: silent cells often enter strong chorus states, recruited taxa deepen across multiple physical sites more coherently than first-order recruitment and spread predict, and historically strong sites preferentially host strong wet-state chorusing.**

## General ecological claim

**A fast environmental gate can reveal higher-order spatial coherence on a slow species × site template in a behaviourally cryptic animal community.**

This is presented as a principle supported in the NAAMP frog system, not as a universal law.

## What makes the paper surprising

The unexpected result is the **conjunction**, not any single percentage:

1. acoustic silence often jumps directly to overlapping/full chorus;
2. the excess response appears after recruited species reach three or more sites;
3. even at the same recruited-taxon count and same total extra-stop spread, extra incidences are too concentrated within the same taxa under uniform, persistence, cross-fit species-response and joint species+strictly-prior-history nulls;
4. that multi-site response is almost entirely strong chorus;
5. the strong sites recur in species-specific historical locations;
6. rain selectively sharpens that historical targeting;
7. even a held-out species + local-history + rain-gating process cannot generate the full allocation.

## Claims intentionally not made

- rain causes the observed response;
- acoustic zero means physical absence;
- individuals return to the same sites;
- the mechanism is biological memory or philopatry;
- strong chorus equals successful spawning;
- sites are demographically connected;
- the same mechanism is universal across anurans;
- the final lower-level generator has been identified.

## Editorial rule

Every paragraph of the integrated manuscript should answer one of four questions:

1. **What changed in frog reproductive acoustic state?**
2. **Was the spatial spread only first-order, or higher-order coherent within the same recruited taxa?**
3. **Did that spatial placement recur historically?**
4. **Which simple ecological explanations failed to generate it?**

Material that does not serve one of these questions belongs in Supporting Information.
