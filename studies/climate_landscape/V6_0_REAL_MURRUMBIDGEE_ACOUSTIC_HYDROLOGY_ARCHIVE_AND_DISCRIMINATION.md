This is **post-plan activity evidence**. The authors report manual checking of **almost 3,000 sound recordings**, machine recognition work and frog temporal responses in four Mid-Murrumbidgee wetlands:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Gooragool | remained dry | frogs largely silent |

| Wilbriggie | filled with environmental water | frog calling increased; barking marsh frog, spotted marsh frog and Peron's tree frog highlighted |

## B. Authentic publicly accessible *matched audio + water* example

**The Sound of Water: Environmental Flows in Nap Nap Swamp**, original public interactive site:

https://media.flow-mer.org.au/napnap/

An open-access account and contextual publication:
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14744740231197813

The source explicitly presents audio clips and a changing water-level visualisation for **nine days (30 August–7 September 2020)** during managed arrival of environmental water at **ONE named wetland**. Selected recorded times are **five-minute segments** (e.g. 06:00–06:05 30 August); it describes first frog calls and changing community chorus as water rises. The scholarly article explains **hourly five-minute audio collection** at the wetland and water-level depth logging.

**What is actually public:** an interactive, curated 9-day sequence of audio/hydrology display and selected sound clips. The entire original archive, exact complete timestamp-level event table, numeric hydrological series, calibration/sensitivity and reproducible machine-readable export have **not** been verified as bulk accessible.

**Admissible use now:** unequivocal proof that time-matched audio and hydrological measurements have already been gathered in the region; a one-wetland source-case feasibility example.

**Forbidden interpretation:** do NOT call this a four- or twelve-site replication, a causal test proving frogs respond to water independent of rain/time, a larval-payoff outcome, or empirical evidence for historical-site targeting.

## D. The discriminating biological question and observable contrast

Existing JAE RC6 NAAMP Q1–Q3:
1. acoustic silence → strong calling;
2. same-taxon multi-stop response deeper than comparator;
3. preferential repeated calling at earlier strong taxon-specific physical sites.

**Do not re-use NAAMP's acoustic CI2/3 label** as if a new audio classifier produced it. Define new audio-unit and acoustic-intensity/state metrics only after an external source dictionary and *before inspecting its new frog outcome panel*.

The actual prospective independent-study question is:

> Does long-term taxon-specific chorus-site recurrence predict **which independently defined wetlands** enter a stronger calling state during a hydrological episode, *after* accounting for wetland water state, clock/solar time, season, site and sampling effort?

Two competing explanations:

- **H0 (hydrological/measurement sufficiency):** wetland-specific water depth/inundation/hydroperiod, season, sound-quality and persistent point propensity predict the response. Historical strong-chorus site history adds no independent out-of-period predictive information.
- **H1 (recurrent spatial selectivity):** with same past/out-of-time site history, wetland water-state, seasonal and effort differences represented, historic species-specific strong-use sites retain **incremental out-of-year predictive value**. This is a predictive conditional allocation result, *not proof of behavioral memory, adaptation or reproductive fitness*.

Freeze ecological-unit/site-year identity, true audio opportunities including valid nondetections, source species detection calibration, legitimate repeated wetland × night panels and independent hydrological chronology BEFORE outcome access. Avoid conditioning on post-exposure observed number active `k` as a causal effect; conditional-k placement can be a separate predictive diagnostic.

Suggested held-out design, only if feasible: historical site-use model on earlier water years → forecast later distinct years and wetlands; compare **weather + local hydrology + site** against **same model + strictly prior within-species acoustic history**; cluster uncertainty by physical wetland and catchment-event, never count clips/hour as independently sampled wetlands. A 4-site, 2-watered/2-dry pilot is not sufficient for general `k≥4` conclusions.

## E. One decisive access request, and scientific STOP

The revised [v6.0 single minimal source inventory request (UNSENT)](V6_0_CEWH_ACOUSTIC_HYDROLOGY_SOURCE_INVENTORY_REQUEST_UNSENT.md) asks **whether a machine-readable, non-sensitive `wetland_alias × recorder × time interval` deployment/effort inventory plus time-varying water and taxon detections exists**; it does NOT request raw audio first, protected coordinates or species occurrences.

Required preliminary *non-outcome* answer:
- how many distinct independently defined wetland units, by monitoring year;
- number of actual dates/intervals with multiple simultaneously active recorders and complete negative recording opportunities, not a notional 12;
- whether station identities are stable through replacement and deployment gaps;
- if actual water depth/inundation timestamps can align to recorder time;
- whether manually validated frog classifier events and per-species false-negative tests/recording effort are preserved and shareable;
- whether a non-sensitive station alias/manifest can be supplied with relevant dataset permissions.

**If no source-authenticated record-year/site opportunity manifest exists, STOP testing landscape Q2/Q3 from that data.** One-site Nap Nap remains a descriptive timing example. Do not retrofit noise/feature counts to a fitness or causal claim. Any human correspondence is NOT SENT here.
