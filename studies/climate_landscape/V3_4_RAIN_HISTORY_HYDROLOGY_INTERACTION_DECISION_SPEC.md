## I. Three distinct kinds of “rain × history” evidence already exist

Keep these observations separate: a conditional-null's inability to explain the full pattern is *not* evidence that the corresponding interaction is absent.

### A — A rain × prior-site-propensity comparator is insufficient to reproduce the spatial endpoint

The 2,916-pair strictly-prior-history dataset contains a principal comparator with cross-fitted taxon-specific rainfall sensitivity, species × SiteID history, dry persistence and matched wet activation magnitude. Observed within-taxon concentration was **1.6503**, compared with **1.3535** predicted; the conditional residual was **0.2969** (P=0.000999 from 1,000 simulations).

The stronger *route-cross-fitted* generator **already includes rain contrast × centered prior species × SiteID probability**, applied to initially silent cells. It predicts **1.3323**, conditional residual **0.3180** (simulated 95% null range −0.1172 to 0.1255; plus-one P=0.000999).

**Interpretation:** that particular first-order rain × historical local-propensity gate does not explain the **joint within-taxon spatial configuration**. It does **not** test whether all biologically possible rain × local habitat/hydrology interactions are absent or prove a unique shared-state generator. (Main manuscript; SI S11.3–S11.4.)

### B — Rain-selective historical strong-chorus placement *is positively associated*

In a directional within-pair placebo, the wet and dry directions were compared using the contrast in CI3 probability between historically strong versus other candidate physical SiteIDs for the same taxon. With signed target rain advantage, the coefficient was **+0.02449** (95% CI **0.00700–0.04198**), and in same-observer sensitivity **+0.03067** (CI **0.01331–0.04804**).

**Interpretation:** rain proximity is associated with preferential full-chorus expression at *previously* strong taxon × physical-site combinations beyond generic recurrence. This is a **directional targeting result**, not direct hydrological measurement, and β is in the specified regression's coefficient units; do not call it a general causal interaction size. (Main manuscript; SI S14.)

### C — Rain-selective alignment is stronger in a deep than shallow spatial state (post-hoc)

In the exact-k q-conditioned placement diagnostic among **409 deep (k≥4) taxon × route-night clusters**, the deep-template alignment rainfall coefficient was **+0.02659**, Monte Carlo **P=0.001998**. Direct deep (k≥4) minus shallow (k=1–3) *rainfall-coefficient* contrast was **+0.02970**, simulated 95% interval **−0.02532 to 0.02889**, **P=0.02098**. The cluster-mean difference was **+0.08685** (P=0.00599).

**Interpretation:** broad activation can coincide with more—not less—site selectivity. But the contrast is **post-hoc on already explored NAAMP outcomes** and not an external replication or proof of the biological source of site selection. (Main manuscript, common-cause falsification synthesis, SI S15.)

### D — Other rain interactions constrain easy stories

The prospective or held-out tests reported in SI include:
- **rain contrast × baseline dryness:** β=+0.0247, CI −0.241 to 0.290, P=0.855; no supported amplification by baseline regional dryness.
- **rain × early response-sign diversity:** β=−0.954, CI −3.267 to 1.359, P=0.419; buffering hypothesis unsupported.
- **rain × early between-stop acoustic β-diversity:** β=−0.513, CI −0.938 to −0.087, P=0.018, **opposite the fixed predicted direction**; exact-consecutive-year β=−0.354, CI −0.854 to 0.145, P=0.165. Do not assert a robust general reversal.
- **rain recency × temperature:** included in a flexible nonlinear environmental generator. Combined flexibility only removed **11.4%** of principal configuration residual; the interaction itself has **not** been isolated as a supported mechanism in this result.
- **antecedent 72-hour precipitation amount:** explains some strong activation, but its increment for configuration was small; a **future-rain negative control** limited attribution specifically to antecedent 72-h amount.

These findings cannot be pooled into one undifferentiated statement that “rain interactions were significant.”

## II. What ecology could generate the positive rain × recurrence?

The leading question is no longer “Does rain trigger frog calls?” but:

> **Does recent rain select a pre-existing taxon × location acoustic template because it makes those local wetlands temporarily usable, or does rain recruit a taxon-wide calling state whose placement retains structure even after local wetness is measured?**

Three generators yield distinct, falsifiable *future-study* expectations:

| Competing generator | Mechanism | Future measurement prediction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 Dynamic local-water availability** | Rain alters which wetlands are inundated/moist or thermally suitable; historically strong sites proxy repeatable hydroperiod/retention | Current validated site-event W/D, level, hydroperiod and water temperature account for much of history targeting and within-route configuration; route-night latent dependence shrinks after current local water state |
| **H2 Shared taxon-night activation × local filtering** | A broad favourable state recruits calling, but only selected local sites express strong chorus | Local water-state improves marginal prediction, but residual taxon×route-night shared covariance remains and **non-uniform** spatial placement survives; shared-state×local-state generator outpredicts either alone |
| **H3 History-dependent biotic/observation process** | Local resident demographic availability, breeding readiness, conspecific calling, observer/detection timing or other unmeasured causes anchor repeat use | Direct wetness adds limited explanatory value; prior taxon×site history and/or prior calling variables retain the placement pattern; observation-time and repeated acoustic sampling distinguish activity from survey artifacts |

A **nonmonotonic “readiness window”** is a further testable variant of H1, not a discovered result: rain might activate previously dry temporary pools, do little at persistently wet sites, or suppress calling if water is excessive. Do not convert this into a convenient after-the-fact threshold or assume W/D is a measure of water depth or reproductive success.

### Close relevant published precedents (context, not verification of frogcs mechanism)

- Brinley Buckley et al. (2021), *Ecological Indicators* 121:107171, paired time-lapse **inundation** with acoustic recordings; hydropattern and recent rainfall were associated with calling.
- Sarker et al. (2022), *Ecological Indicators*, studied chorus changes surrounding **actual wetland inundation**, showing that river-flow and local habitat processes are biologically relevant.
- Brodie et al. (2025), *Ecosphere*, examined rain lag and species-specific chorusing across wet seasons in an Australian tropical savanna.
- *Managing flows for frogs* (2024), *Marine and Freshwater Research*, distinguished **inundation extent associated with calling** from accumulated flow associated with **breeding success**, underscoring that calls do not prove recruitment.
- *Automated Acoustic Recogniser Reveals Spatial Variation ... Magnificent Broodfrog* (2026): rainfall associations even differed in **sign** across regions; local buffering is offered as a possible explanation rather than measured causal hydrology.

Together these studies make rain×local habitat biologically plausible but do **not** identify frogcs's missing mechanism or provide replication of its conditional taxon-by-site spatial endpoint.

### B. Separate marginal response from configuration

For each taxon at stop `i`, route-night `t`, retain an ordinal acoustic response (CI 0–3 or a validated comparable scale). Define at least:

1. **Magnitude outcome:** conditional probability of strong calling `CI≥2`, at the sampled surveyed stop. Account for stop observation duration, observer, background noise, exact survey time, season and genuine skipped sites.
2. **Configuration outcome:** among a taxon's observed `k` calling-positive or strong stops, their positions across **physical** sites; compare to a frozen, *out-of-block* site-level generator **conditional on k**. This is not automatically determined by the increase in total strong calling.
3. **Joint dependence:** within-taxon route-night dependence/near–far profile and nonuniformity. A model reproducing total k but making all stops too coherent **fails** this endpoint.

Do not infer actual spawning, abundance, movement, memory, individual return, or demographic colonization from CI alone.

### C. The scientific interaction contrast

Let `R_t` represent **temporally measured rain before observation**, `W_it` direct local wetness, and `H_{qi}` the **strictly prior** strong-chorus history for taxon q at physical site i, constructed without leakage into the evaluation period.

**Primary predictive competition:**
- weather/phenology + prior taxon×site history (**baseline**);
- actual local wetland state + weather/phenology + prior history;
- local wetland state + **taxon-night shared state × current local water state**, with prior history.

**Separate interpretive contrasts:** check whether rain-associated historical targeting attenuates after actual W/D, water depth and hydroperiod are added; whether `R×W` adds held-out predictive value; and whether `R×H` remains predictive. The historical targeting term is not a causal “memory” effect. Because current W is plausibly **downstream of R**, controlling for it changes the estimand: a reduced R/H coefficient supports a possible *mediating explanation* only under additional assumptions, never causal mediation by itself.

When including **route-night fixed effects**, a route-night-constant rainfall main effect is not separately identifiable. Cross-night changes in rainfall interactions with site-varying W/H may still be estimable with sufficient crossed observations. Check design rank *after* all site/taxon/history/season strata and missing observations are represented; the source-only W/D graph diagnostic is **necessary but insufficient** for the complete fitted model.

### D. Independent holdout and stop rule

Freeze the complete eligible geography/time/taxa, splitting logic, W/D semantics, the operational lag(s), comparator families, the fixed-k conditioned placement endpoint, positive/negative controls, joint spatial prediction score and an informativeness gate **before reading focal frog responses**. One predeclared out-of-block evaluation (e.g., held-out routes/years) is primary; no post-result searches for favourable species, rain windows, stop subsets or 2012 forest-loss locations. Report inconclusive if identity, W/D support or cross-block breadth fails. Classify any analyses of frozen RC6 outcomes as exploratory only.

**The causal result is not yet known.** Existing data support **rain-selective historical acoustic placement** and extra deep spatial coupling, but they do not discriminate H1/H2/H3. The next informative measurement is direct event-varying wetland state together with repeat acoustic activity on truly stable sites.
