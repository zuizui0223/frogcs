# Route-topology geometry interpretation v0.1

## Stage-A result

The prespecified primary hypothesis was:

> deep k>=4 activation should retain excess route-order holes after exact k and fixed-q site propensity are represented.

That prediction **failed**.

Primary deep holes excess:
- deep clusters: **767**
- routes: **243**
- observed mean excess: **−0.1535**
- exact-k null 95% interval: **−0.0818 to 0.0838**
- prespecified upper-tail P: **1.0**
- route-bootstrap 95% interval: **−0.2578 to −0.0494**
- primary support classification: **FAIL**

The failure is in the opposite direction from the prediction.

## Fixed secondary geometry metrics

All fixed secondary outputs point toward **more compact route-order geometry** than the fixed-q exact-k comparator predicts:

- span excess: **−0.1535**
- component excess: **−0.1210**
- mean pairwise StopNumber-lag excess: **−0.0987**

The secondary two-sided exact-k null tests place each observed value outside its null 95% interval.

Deep-cluster weighted means:
- observed holes ≈ **2.486** vs q-expected **2.640**
- observed span ≈ **7.433** vs q-expected **7.586** stop steps
- observed contiguous components ≈ **2.519** vs q-expected **2.640**
- observed mean pairwise lag ≈ **3.552** vs q-expected **3.650**

These effects are modest in magnitude but directionally coherent.

## Ecological interpretation

The landscape extension therefore does **not** support a picture in which deep rain-associated activation becomes unusually fragmented or scattered along the route.

Instead, conditional on:
- exact observed activation depth k;
- the strongest fixed-q site propensities;

deep active sets are slightly **more compact and contiguous in route order** than expected.

This modifies the working landscape picture:

> **broad activation does not imply spatial homogenization, but neither is it unusually scattered; it is consistent with route-spanning activity expressed through relatively compact activation domains.**

This is compatible with the already established facts that:
- deep k>=4 expression exists more often than activation nulls predict;
- far route-position dependence remains positive;
- a uniform all-stop scalar state is too coherent;
- recurrent strong-chorus sites are preferentially involved in deep activation.

## What this does not show

The Stage-A geometry result does not identify:
- geographic distance;
- hydrological connectivity;
- movement or propagation;
- acoustic contagion;
- individual fidelity;
- a causal landscape mechanism.

StopNumber remains route topology / survey order, not exact Euclidean distance.

> **Deep activation may form compact route-order domains around recurrent strong-chorus sites.**

This must be tested as a new Stage-A2 hypothesis, not retroactively treated as the original Stage-A prediction.

The Stage-A2 test must condition on:

The primary Stage-A2 metric will measure the route-order distance from active non-strong sites to active prior-strong sites.
