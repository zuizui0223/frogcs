## 1. Scientific positioning — the one generalizable claim

**What we demonstrably show within the frozen model family:**

> A mechanism that makes an interaction-like signature easier to generate need not increase the focal species' abundance or long-term occupancy. Which demographic effect occurs depends on the protected prey pathway and developmental arrival/duration.

This is a model-level **counterexample to naive inference** from directionally compatible interaction signatures to positive demographic benefit, not a field-demonstrated conservation consequence.

**Separate evidence:**
- **R1 actual observation**: caudate-suitability conditions an odonate-suitability versus adult frog calling association across 4,248 NAAMP locations (uniform high−low −0.385; target-group −0.179). Shared-environment/G1 and G2B heldout analyses did **not** establish robust additional O×S predictability in both backgrounds. No wild predation effect identified.
- **G4–G6 field–model correspondence**: source-constrained model11 slopes were positive in every original training fold under both SDM specifications. The retrospective G6 decomposition locates the positive point improvements over the nonlinear environmental baseline in the **506 landscape-vegetation-recorded sites** (NLL gain per visit **+0.006268 / +0.003091**); the remaining 3,742 sites had near-zero or slightly negative gains. Observed and predicted R1 rank contrasts also become less negative in the vegetation-recorded group. This is a concrete conditional correspondence, with uncertainty retained: group and group-difference intervals include zero, the simple additive environmental model remains better overall, and G4/G5's original gates remain unmet. G6 does not validate P1's natural timing mechanism or select a field predator pathway.
- **R2 model prior**: 794/20,000 original model proposals reproduced idealized log aquatic frog directions (OS− OV− SV+ OSV+), with rate-region selection performed *after* examining the original proposal grid. Thus the source fractions are not field-likelihood ABC posterior weights.
- **R2 model intervention**: in independent new seed **256 matched legal canonical backgrounds**, simultaneous frog shelter from salamanders and vegetation-supported odonate feeding raised four-sign compatibility **3.52%→14.06%**, source-cluster paired +10.55 percentage points and factorial interaction **+4.04 pp [0.91,7.29]**.
- **R3 model-demographic divergence**: the D→F share of total S→F plus D→F hazard is calculated **source-by-source before averaging**, and that mean share increased from ~59.7% to ~73.9%, **as assumed mechanistic hazards responded**; mean log F fell **−0.0841 [−0.1128,−0.0557]** under both functions despite the compatibility gain. This is not a ratio of two separately averaged hazards.
- **P1 isolated timing and aquatic exposure**: truly isolated S arrival day0/day40 and S aquatic duration40/60 (D duration fixed60) in **4,096 original 80-year biology scenarios** with the same original 256 backgrounds. Relative to plants OFF, both ON yielded Δlog F **−0.1864** late/short, **−0.1860** late/long, **+0.0021** early/short and **+0.1163** early/long. Meanwhile corresponding four-sign compatibility *benefits* **+13.28,+10.94,+9.38,+2.34 pp**. Earlier S arrival alone reduces the negative log F effect to approximately zero; **prolonged aquatic duration is also necessary** for a positive log F benefit within this model. Original context1/2 replays were independently verified at 2,048 entire source models and 16,384 vertices.
- **R4 model population**: same new seed 256 model backgrounds in **24,576 truly stochastic 80-year finite-N arms**. q3 movement-ON overall net occupancy gain from both plant functions **+0.029 [−0.037,+0.096]** artificial patches (not distinguishable from zero); q3 plant factorial occupancy +0.100 [0.018,0.180] patches; mean count change −5.325 [−7.205,−3.537] frogs per artificial eight-patch ring. External immigration retained ON, so return events are NOT a field dispersal-rescue estimate.
- **G3 field phenology STOP**: GBIF egg-stage species-coverage gate failed (2/15 original frogs and 1/5 pond caudates with >=50 egg reports each; preregistered minimum 5/15 and 3/5). No NAAMP vegetation×natural breeding-priority regression or pure field phenology confirmation was done.

**Distinction with previous research:** Blanchet, Cazelles & Gravel (2020, *Ecology Letters* 23:1050–1063, https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13525) argue that co-occurrence should not be interpreted as proof of interactions. Our question is a further inference step: *even when an ecological model reproduces a target interaction-like signature, demographic consequences need not follow from the signature*. This is a distinct question, but our NAAMP response is **acoustic activity vs environmental suitability, not measured co-occurrence**, and the theoretical counterexample is tested in one specified food-web model family. Hossie & Murray (2010) showed that leaf litter changes the density dependence of dragonfly predation on tadpoles, while Mocq et al. (2021) found vegetation-assisted dragonfly attack on planktonic invertebrate prey. The latter prey were not tadpoles. These studies motivate contrasting model habitat functions without calibrating their magnitudes or validating their simultaneous effects in the frog/odonate/salamander model; see the [verified references and claim boundaries](DATA_CODE_AND_REFERENCE_PROVENANCE_2026-11-06.md).

## 6. Submission order and stopping rule

At the present evidence level, **scientific fit: Oikos > GEB ≈ Ecology Letters**; *aspirational impact*: EL > GEB/Oikos. Editorial outcomes cannot be predicted numerically from journal metrics. A one-shot EL attempt is strategically reasonable only if journal fit is considered adequate by coauthors and the manuscript explicitly names its field/model gap. If avoiding likely desk rejection is the primary goal, **start with Oikos**. The manuscript should remain one integrated paper throughout; a rejection should trigger journal-specific framing, not aggressive deletion of failed G1 or artificial changes of source priors.
