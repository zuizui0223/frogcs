Dear Editors,

We would like to submit our Letter, **“Predator timing reverses vegetation effects in a stage-structured food web,”** for consideration in *Ecology Letters*.

Habitat structure can affect prey through shelter and feeding opportunities for different predators. We ask a concrete mechanistic question: **when can predator development reverse the net demographic effect of these opposing habitat functions?**

In a controlled factorial across 256 matched food-web model backgrounds, independently varying salamander arrival and aquatic-stage duration changed the combined vegetation effect on mean log frog abundance from −0.186 under late arrival/short duration to +0.116 under early arrival/long duration. Early arrival largely removed the negative effect, and prolonged aquatic presence then produced a positive response. The positive arrival×duration interaction identifies a joint developmental condition for the reversal. The two vegetation functions also increased four-sign interaction-pattern compatibility from 3.52% to 14.06%, with positive nonadditivity overall. The condition providing the greatest abundance benefit produced the smallest compatibility gain. Across 24,576 stochastic 80-year simulations, a positive occupancy effect occurred in the early-salamander context at low count scale with movement, while pooled occupancy benefits remained uncertain.

The empirical motivation is a caudate-suitability-dependent relationship between odonate suitability and adult frog calling across 4,248 repeatedly surveyed North American locations. Field prediction tests did not discriminate the modelled mechanism: gains from a fixed model response were uncertain, simpler environmental models predicted better, and a prespecified species-group comparison did not improve geographical prediction. These results retain the observed conditional association while setting the boundary of the mechanistic claim.

The contribution is a specific, testable account of **when predator development makes opposing habitat functions beneficial to prey**, together with evidence that the demographic outcome and the interaction signature favour different conditions. The controlled interventions establish this result within the stated model family and identify the measurements needed to test it in natural ponds.

The current main manuscript file contains **4,956 words** including its abstract, headings, captions and status material; the abstract is **137 words**. The review package includes **four quantitative main-figure candidates**, supplementary G6/G7 diagnostic figures and a post-hoc P1 decomposition figure. The latter attributes the reversal to stronger refuge benefits and weaker feeding-support costs; all 256 backgrounds and all timing conditions are retained. Full original-source analyses, negative tests, model-rate sensitivity and observational limitations are documented in the accompanying Supplementary Information. Verified primary references and data provenance accompany the draft, including the original numerical inputs needed for claim replay.
