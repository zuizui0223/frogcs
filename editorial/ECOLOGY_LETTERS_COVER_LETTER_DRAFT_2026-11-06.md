Dear Editors,

We would like to submit our Letter, **“Food webs that reproduce frog–predator association signs need not benefit frogs,”** for consideration in *Ecology Letters*.

Regional species associations can suggest trophic interactions, but multiple processes can generate similar directional signatures. We ask an ecological question that is rarely distinguished in pattern-oriented models: **does a food web's ability to generate an interaction-like pattern also imply a demographic benefit to the focal organism?**

Across 4,248 repeatedly surveyed North American locations, adult frog calling showed a conditional association with odonate and caudate environmental suitability. We then screened 20,000 proposals spanning 3,600 canonical stage-structured food webs and hypothetical ecological rates, finding 794 that generated the four idealized association directions. At a historically explored rate setting, the controlled 256-background vegetation-function factorial raised four-sign compatibility from 3.52% to 14.06%. Crucially, an additional prospectively fixed experiment across **all 27 attack–recruitment–conversion rate cells**, retaining 256 identical food-web backgrounds and all intervention arms, found a mean compatibility gain of **+2.71 percentage points** (paired-background interval +2.02 to +3.41) while mean log aquatic frog abundance **decreased by −0.040** (interval −0.049 to −0.031). Positive signature nonadditivity was not general across the rate grid.

A post-hoc, complete joint enumeration found that **571 of 782 (73.0%)** model background–rate–context comparisons in which vegetation functions *newly generated* all four signs nevertheless showed decreased simulated frog cohorts. Importantly, a further **rate×developmental-context-standardized comparison** did not show a clear *excess* of losses relative to other model structures under the same conditions (**−4.93 percentage points**, source-bootstrap interval **−11.28 to +0.83**). The relative association changed direction across developmental contexts and hypothetical attack regimes. Neither compatibility nor the subsequent demographic outcome is a measured field trophic effect.

The population consequence was nevertheless conditional, not uniformly harmful. A separate controlled salamander arrival×aquatic-duration factorial reversed the combined vegetation effect from **−0.186** (late/short) to **+0.116** (early/long). Across the broader 27-rate experiment, the original early/long developmental context produced a positive average frog-cohort response (+0.025), whereas the other two original contexts were negative. Thus, **pattern-generation ability, abundance effects and developmental contingencies are different ecological outcomes**.

We distinguish this model-based discovery from field identification. The observed variables are environmental suitability and adult calling rather than measured pond predator densities or larval survival. The four-sign target is idealized; model-derived predictors did not outperform a simple environmental acoustic model on held-out geographical blocks, and the species-group test was inconclusive. Accordingly, we do not claim to have uniquely identified the natural trophic pathway or calibrated the sign-generating model to the magnitude of observed correlations. The contribution is a tested limitation of interpreting geographical association patterns mechanistically: even plausible sign-generating food webs may reduce the focal population.

**Private editorial notes (not to include in letter):** G10 is post hoc and proves no relative loss excess overall after adjusting hypothetical rates and original developmental context. The 73% G9 descriptive frequency must never be represented as a causal harm from matching the signs.  The main risk is that the 4,248-site field result does not discriminate a particular trophic mechanism beyond both geographic SDM backgrounds; G8 is an internally controlled rate-generalization test, not field calibration. The original positive fixed-rate nonadditivity is not general across all 27 rates. Do not write that the model **explains** the NAAMP association or that the GBIF stage facet yielded breeding onset. The attack/recruitment regime was historically selected from a synthetic model prior; the independent 256-background test validates only conditional model generality. In the full 27-rate experiment the pooled sign-compatibility contrast is positive but only 9/27 individual pointwise cell intervals are positive, with 3 negative; do not claim rate-universal synergy.
