Dear Editors,

We would like to submit our Letter, **“Food webs that reproduce frog–predator association signs need not benefit frogs,”** for consideration in *Ecology Letters*.

Regional species associations can suggest trophic interactions, but multiple processes can generate similar directional signatures. We ask an ecological question that is rarely distinguished in pattern-oriented models: **does a food web's ability to generate an interaction-like pattern also imply a demographic benefit to the focal organism?**

Across 4,248 repeatedly surveyed North American locations, adult frog calling showed a conditional association with odonate and caudate environmental suitability. We then performed a rejection ABC-style POM screen of **20,000 synthetic proposals** spanning 3,600 canonical stage-structured food webs and hypothetical ecological rates. **794 proposals across 647 distinct canonical model IDs** generated the four idealized association directions. Reweighting nine illustrative synthetic rate priors shifted the apparent model-native acceptance rate from **1.09% to 6.85%**; stronger minimum effect thresholds also reduced acceptance. A separate **81-rate/context-stratum conditional-null test** found significantly fewer accepted distinct model IDs than expected if structure did not matter after rate/context stratification (**647 observed versus 712 null mean; 95% null range 696–727; permutation p=0.0002**). This demonstrates **synthetic model-catalogue structural selectivity beyond rate and developmental context**, even though 647 candidates remain and no empirically calibrated Bayesian posterior has been obtained. At a historically explored rate setting, the controlled 256-background vegetation-function factorial raised four-sign compatibility from 3.52% to 14.06%. Crucially, an additional prospectively fixed experiment across **all 27 attack–recruitment–conversion rate cells**, retaining 256 identical food-web backgrounds and all intervention arms, found a mean compatibility gain of **+2.71 percentage points** (paired-background interval +2.02 to +3.41) while mean log aquatic frog abundance **decreased by −0.040** (interval −0.049 to −0.031). Positive signature nonadditivity was not general across the rate grid. A **fully disjoint second sample of 256 previously untested legal food-web structures** reproduced both pre-fixed original model directions across all 27 rate cells: four-sign compatibility **+2.75 points [2.08,3.43]**, mean frog log cohorts **−0.041 [−0.050,−0.032]**. Its 64 original 80-year shards and eight-corner source states passed a complete independent source audit.

The disjoint structural holdout likewise had **612 of 835 (73.3%)** new-sign cases with lower frog cohorts, without implying excess harm over appropriate rate/context comparators. A post-hoc, complete joint enumeration found that **571 of 782 (73.0%)** model background–rate–context comparisons in which vegetation functions *newly generated* all four signs nevertheless showed decreased simulated frog cohorts. Importantly, a further **rate×developmental-context-standardized comparison** did not show a clear *excess* of losses relative to other model structures under the same conditions (**−4.93 percentage points**, source-bootstrap interval **−11.28 to +0.83**). The relative association changed direction across developmental contexts and hypothetical attack regimes. Neither compatibility nor the subsequent demographic outcome is a measured field trophic effect.

The population consequence was nevertheless conditional, not uniformly harmful. A separate controlled salamander arrival×aquatic-duration factorial reversed the combined vegetation effect from **−0.186** (late/short) to **+0.116** (early/long). Across the broader 27-rate experiment, the original early/long developmental context produced a positive average frog-cohort response (+0.025), whereas the other two original contexts were negative. Thus, **pattern-generation ability, abundance effects and developmental contingencies are different ecological outcomes**.

We distinguish this model-based discovery from field identification. The observed variables are environmental suitability and adult calling rather than measured pond predator densities or larval survival. The four-sign target is idealized. Earlier G5/G7 model-derived constraints did not establish robust gains over the environmental alternatives. The new G14 test found that the original 794-proposal ABC-accepted mean interaction template modestly improved geographically heldout prediction against a flexible additive environmental model with **uniform** SDM backgrounds (**+0.001155 NLL per completed visit**, block interval +0.000253 to +0.002427). The result was uncertain under **target-group** SDMs (+0.000163, interval −0.000548 to +0.000931), and the template did not robustly beat unrestricted O/S/V interactions under either background. Its pre-fixed two-background gates therefore **failed**. An additional G15 specificity test against **199 rate×developmental-context-matched source-selection nulls** showed the original ABC ensemble outperforming **199/199 nulls under uniform SDMs but 0/199 under target-group SDMs**; it nevertheless improved over a single arbitrary sign-only control in both backgrounds. A full original-source G16 decomposition traced **95.6% of that background difference to sites without mapped flooded vegetation**, while no single geographic block caused the ranking reversal. The two SDM backgrounds also ranked odonate and caudate suitability differently. G17's pre-fixed diagonal numerical replay gate failed and was retained as such. A distinct post-hoc G17b test using exact archived G15 diagonal predictions showed that the ABC-versus-199-null ranking followed the **odonate/caudate suitability-rank source** in both additive environmental backgrounds, rather than the environmental model background itself. These are changes of statistical predictor representation, not measurements of local predator populations or evidence of a natural trophic mechanism. Accordingly, we do not claim to have uniquely identified the natural trophic pathway or calibrated the sign-generating model to the magnitude of observed correlations. An external USGS field monitoring dataset (Glorioso & Waddle 2025, DOI 10.5066/P9T71JCO) permitted direct alignment of adult vocalization and tadpole detection across 1,054 same-sample surveys. The observed within-sample detection association was positive, but adding earlier adult calls did not reliably improve later larval detection across sites and actually worsened fixed forward-year prediction. These independently collected source data therefore make the observation-process limitation explicit rather than supplying a field-mechanism posterior. The contribution is a tested limitation of interpreting geographical association patterns mechanistically: even plausible sign-generating food webs may reduce the focal population.
