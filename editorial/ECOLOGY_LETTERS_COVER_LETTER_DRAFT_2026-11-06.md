Dear Editors,

We would like to submit our Letter, **“Interaction signatures need not track population benefits in stage-structured food webs,”** for consideration in *Ecology Letters*.

Ecologists increasingly recognize that large-scale species associations cannot, on their own, identify direct interactions. We address a complementary and potentially more consequential inference problem: **even when a biologically explicit mechanism reproduces an interaction-like signature, does that achievement tell us whether the mechanism supports the focal population?**

We first document a caudate-suitability-dependent relationship between odonate suitability and adult frog calling across 4,248 repeatedly surveyed North American monitoring locations. A fixed mechanism-derived response shape connects the model to geographic holdouts: a retrospective diagnosis locates its positive predictive point gains in the 506 locations with recorded landscape flooded vegetation, although the intervals include zero and simpler environmental models predict better. We then investigate the downstream theoretical problem using stage-structured food webs. In 256 newly and independently sampled, legally matched model backgrounds, two vegetation-mediated predator pathways increased four-sign interaction-pattern compatibility from 3.52% to 14.06% while reducing mean modelled frog abundance. In a further 2×2 factorial that isolates salamander arrival from aquatic-stage duration, the developmental setting with the largest benefit to frog abundance exhibited the smallest gain in signature compatibility. Stochastic 80-year population simulations on the same model backgrounds did not consistently translate pattern compatibility into a net occupancy benefit.

We believe this distinction advances ecological interpretation beyond the established caution that co-occurrence alone cannot prove interactions: it demonstrates **why the ability to generate a plausible interaction signature is an insufficient criterion for evaluating its demographic consequences**. The work integrates a broad observed motivating pattern, independent within-model ecological interventions, predator-pathway diagnostics and matched population outcomes without equating them or claiming unsupported causal identification in the field.
