# Prior larvae to subsequent chorus: exploratory design

Written before reading the prior-exposure/outcome cross-tabulations. This is a new exploratory analysis, not passage of the older frozen 30-opportunity eligibility rule. Preserve that rule and its outputs.

Use the two original checksum-pinned INBO archives and exact publisher location IDs, with locality consistency checks. Export aggregates only. The focal response is explicit adult Hyla arborea presence in the chorus protocol, not strong-call frequency, recruitment, or oviposition. The exposure is explicit larval presence/absence, separately for Hyla and each recorded caudate/anuran species. A missing species record is never absence.

Primary lag: one calendar year. Sensitivity: two and three calendar years, reported separately without selecting a favorable lag. Use any recorded presence within each sampled site-year; retain event counts and earliest-survey sensitivity because annual detection depends on survey effort. Compare subsequent chorus proportions after sampled presence vs sampled absence, with site-resampled percentile 95% intervals (4,000 replicates, fixed seed). Report cells and invalid bootstrap replicates; do not force an estimate when a group is missing.

For each contrast also report the earliest sampled event per year, the subset with at least two adult surveys, and comparisons stratified by chorus status in the exposure year. This is descriptive history control, not causal adjustment. Show within-site exposure switcher counts; do not imply a within-site effect from a between-site comparison. Do not pool species absences into guild absence. No model selection or predictive-gain criterion. Small groups and non-estimable contrasts remain in the results.

This temporal test does not measure tadpole survival, adult recruitment, predator consumption, vegetation structure, or individual pond selection. A population feedback simulation can establish consequences conditional on assumptions, but cannot supply those missing observations.
