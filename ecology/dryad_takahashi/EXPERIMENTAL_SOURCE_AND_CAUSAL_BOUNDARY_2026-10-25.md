# Independent egg predation experimental source/estimand contract
Date 2026-10-25. This is a follow-up, exploratory synthesis to existing data, NOT a new experiment or a new confirmatory test.

Source: Takahashi & Ruszala (2023), *Egg predators improve the hatching success of salamander eggs*, Ecology and Evolution, DOI 10.1002/ece3.10427; original publicly listed Dryad dataset DOI 10.5061/dryad.bnzs7h4gn, **version 4**. Published Dryad raw data filename `Hatch_w.csv` (1,017 bytes), file id **2551841**, SHA256 **8bf8166185fb1607710bd3bfd9a81d9f82ea34c1d8ae8874f562d5bee70076db**. Public Dryad README (id 2551847) SHA256 **116219290c72d16b18f3e8def3c4ac707464cbd7f27f796ed5977a7848f0857d**. Never call HTML text or tables a verified original file.

**Known from published paper:** A 2×4 factorial design with intact vs removed jelly and zero/low/medium/high wood-frog tadpole density; 32 spotted-salamander egg masses, 4 egg masses per treatment cell. The **egg mass / rearing container**, not individual hatchlings, is the replicate. The authors already demonstrated a jelly×tadpole-density interaction and counterintuitively highest hatching with intact jelly under high experimental predator density (published mean 91.1% ± 0.09). No novel experimental effect is claimed.

**Raw-data gate:** Attempt only documented official Dryad original file-download endpoints over HTTPS without bypassing authentication. Validate SHA256 byte-for-byte before opening. If all endpoints return access errors, publish a blocker receipt and DO NOT fabricate egg-mass-level rows.

**If original rows become available:** audit raw field labels, exact 8 treatment combinations, n=4 independent egg masses per cell, bounded hatching success 0–1, missing incubation values in predation treatments with zero hatchlings, and any corrected boundary >1 from denominator estimation. Report mean hatching per treatment and four predator-density × jelly interaction contrasts. Use original egg masses as independent units. A sensitivity may use equal-mass treatment cell means and exact interval summaries; no opportunistic post-hoc selection, no invented variance. The full model must not treat 1,401 individual hatchlings as independent treatment replicates.

**Cross-dataset inference:** Independent Mohonk 2016–2021 first-detection and co-stage records establish recurring encounter opportunities only. The controlled lab data establish a context-dependent consequence of exposure only **under the lab light/temperature/predator/egg jelly conditions**. Combining them is mechanistic triangulation; it **does not** establish field predation in Mohonk, egg success, or a quantified field effect. Do not extrapolate the lab 91.1% improvement to 31 observed same-day stage encounters.

**Biological question still not answered:** Does reproductive-stage overlap *causally* alter salamander embryo success in ponds with different dissolved oxygen, vegetated canopy, water depth and frog tadpole density? This needs a matched pond×date predation/hatching study.
