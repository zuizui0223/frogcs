# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.11

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated expansion of frog active communities crosses spatial and taxonomic boundaries without practical homogenization,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling and observed richness are already well established. We therefore ask a broader community-ecological question: **when a short environmental pulse enlarges a behaviourally realized community, does it simply amplify the same participants, or does it recruit new spatial and taxonomic participation?**

Using 4,236 wetter–drier comparisons from 585 NAAMP routes in 21 states, we addressed three linked questions. First, recent-rain surveys had more active stops, higher richness within active stops and higher route-level richness. Second, the resulting species × stop allocation differed from a frozen magnitude-matched uniform-activation null. That null already predicted 80.9% boundary crossing (95% interval 74.2–87.4%), but the observed share was 92.0%; the full four-component allocation rejected uniform activation under all three pre-frozen smoothing specifications (Monte Carlo P = .001 each). Corner expansion and taxonomic deepening were overrepresented, whereas within-core rearrangement was 8.0% observed versus 19.1% expected. Third, pairwise Sørensen differentiation remained practically stable within a fixed post hoc ±0.025 slope margin in both the primary and exact-consecutive-year samples.

The result therefore distinguishes **community amplification** from **community recruitment**. A pulse can increase overall acoustic activity mechanically, yet the observed community expanded through more route-new taxonomic participation and less core rearrangement than that amplification process predicted. At the same time, local compositional differentiation was retained rather than practically homogenized.

The headline pattern was robust to recorded hearing/noise/wind conditions, omission of any single state and exclusion of comparisons confined to the programme’s 0–3-day rain-target window. Rainfall timing was observational, so we do not infer causality. Species-level mechanism also remains unresolved: a proposed activation-geometry trait failed a frozen placebo gate and was removed from the headline rather than retuned.

We believe this fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses a familiar behavioural response to reveal a more general principle: **environmental pulses can recruit previously inactive spatial and taxonomic participation beyond uniform amplification without erasing local differentiation.**

Sincerely,

The authors
