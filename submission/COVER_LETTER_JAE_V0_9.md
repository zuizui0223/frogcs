# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.9

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated expansion of frog active communities crosses spatial and taxonomic boundaries without practical homogenization,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling are well known. Prior work has already reported lagged rainfall associations with frog calling activity and observed species richness (Xie et al., 2017), fine-temporal changes in calling-assemblage composition and beta diversity (Sugai et al., 2021), and wetting/inundation-associated changes in frog richness, chorusing and community composition (Sarker et al., 2022). We do not claim any of those phenomena as new. Instead, we ask a narrower community-ecological question: **where does a short rainfall-associated increase enter a spatially replicated animal community?** Does it simply intensify activity at the same sites, activate additional local communities, deepen diversity within already-active sites, homogenize communities across space, or expand several dimensions simultaneously?

We analysed 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys using 4,236 wetter–drier comparisons from the same route and seasonal sampling window. Each complete route contains ten aligned local sampling stops, allowing the observed acoustic community to be resolved into active spatial footprint, local alpha richness, route gamma richness, among-active-site beta diversity and the underlying species × stop incidence matrix.

Rainfall contrast predicted **more active stops, higher richness within active stops and higher route-level active richness**. The key structural result is now benchmarked against an explicit null. A frozen uniform-activation simulation, calibrated pair by pair to the observed wetter-run incidence magnitude in expectation while preserving dry-side species × stop propensities, predicted a mean boundary-crossing share of 80.9% (95% null interval 74.2–87.4%). The observed share was **92.0%**, and the four-component allocation rejected uniform activation under the primary and both pre-frozen smoothing sensitivities (Monte Carlo P = .001 each). Corner expansion and taxonomic deepening were overrepresented, while within-core rearrangement was 8.0% observed versus 19.1% expected.

The multiscale result was robust to recorded survey conditions, geographic omission and the programme's rain-targeting window. Adjusting for hearing impairment, major-noise timeouts and wind retained positive coefficients for active-stop number, local alpha and route gamma; the same conclusion held after adding traffic counts and in the Massachusetts noise-index subset. In a separately frozen geographic-generality audit, all three rainfall coefficients retained 95% confidence intervals above zero in every one of 21 leave-one-state-out refits. A further frozen design sensitivity retained 1,769 pairs whose drier survey occurred at least four days after rain; footprint, alpha and gamma coefficients again had 95% confidence intervals above zero. State-specific slopes remained heterogeneous, and NAAMP rainfall timing was not randomized, so we interpret these results as robustness to single-state leverage and confinement to the 0–3 day target window rather than as causal evidence.

The beta-diversity wording is also now bounded by an explicit practical margin rather than by non-significance alone. A post hoc margin of ±0.025 Sørensen-slope units was frozen before equivalence readback. The primary 90% interval (-0.0107 to +0.00968) and the exact-consecutive-year interval (-0.00973 to +0.0140) both lay fully inside that margin. We therefore use “without practical homogenization” specifically for pairwise Sørensen within this fixed bound, not as a claim of exact invariance of every beta metric.

Species-level follow-ups were used as falsification tests rather than promoted into the main mechanism. A proposed “activation geometry” was highly repeatable across time and disjoint route sets, but a separately frozen placebo gate showed nearly the same species ranking for reverse-direction gains (rho = 0.929), low-rain-contrast pairs (rho = 0.953) and baseline solitude tendency (rho = 0.782). We therefore removed geometry from the title and main novelty claim rather than residualizing or retuning it. Functional and other static-trait analyses likewise remain secondary.

The manuscript is explicitly limited to the **behaviourally realized acoustic community**. We do not infer occupancy change, colonization, extinction, demographic connectivity among stops or rainfall causality. The original programme-level rainfall endpoint was frozen before readback; the community-scale decompositions were developed subsequently, and each was separately versioned and frozen before its own endpoint readback. Secondary and rejected mechanism tests are retained transparently in Supporting Information rather than used to retune the main story.

We believe the study fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses a familiar behavioural response to answer a broader animal-community question: how can a short environmental pulse enlarge the behaviourally realized community, and is that expansion distinguishable from simply turning up every species × site detection probability by the same amount? The answer is now explicit: uniform activation explains much of the raw boundary crossing, but not its observed allocation. Rainfall-associated growth is more taxonomically recruiting and less core-rearranging than that null predicts, while pairwise Sørensen differentiation remains practically stable within a fixed bound.

Sincerely,

The authors
