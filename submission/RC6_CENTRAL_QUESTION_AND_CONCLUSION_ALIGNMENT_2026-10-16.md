## One biological question (one inferential endpoint)

> **When rain-associated conditions favour frog reproductive calling, at what spatial unit is acoustic activity expressed — as independent local responses, or as selective multi-site activation within the same taxon?**

The focal endpoint is **conditional within-taxon multi-site concentration among acoustically recruited taxa**. Q1 and Q3 give that single endpoint biological context; they are *not* three co-primary inferential endpoints.

| Evidence role | Natural-history question | Supported answer |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 — context | Does calling merely intensify, or do silent taxon × site combinations enter chorus? | Rain-associated conditions often accompany acoustic silence → overlapping or full chorus (CI2/3). This does **not** establish physical arrival or spawning. |
| **Q2 — focal inference** | Are newly expressed calls independently distributed across the 10 stops after the lower-order predictors are represented? | **No under the tested first-order null**: observations retain stronger within-taxon deep multi-site concentration. |

## Reader-facing ecological conclusion

> **Rain-associated chorus activation is neither adequately described by independent local responses nor by a uniform route-wide switch. The same taxa express an unusually deep multi-site acoustic response, and strong calling preferentially recurs at previously used physical sites. The amount of calling and its realised configuration are distinct empirical properties.**

**Scale boundary:** “spatial unit” is inferred from the ten standardized listening positions, the frozen conditional concentration null and post-hoc lag diagnostics. It does *not* identify a breeding population, movement network, physically measured habitat corridor, simultaneous calling or a specific landscape scale in metres. A uniform route-wide scalar alternative proved too coherent in post-hoc diagnostics, not in a newly prespecified causal experiment.

**Environmental boundary:** association with rain recency is not randomized rainfall causation. Local water level/hydroperiod, water temperature, substrate wetness and other fine-scale context were not measured sufficiently to identify a mechanism.
