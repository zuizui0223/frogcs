# Novelty audit v0.8 — RC8 amplification versus recruitment

## Reviewer-safe central advance

Rainfall effects on frog calling, acoustic activity and observed richness are established. Short-term acoustic-community composition and beta diversity are established. Wetting and inundation effects on frog richness and community composition are established. A high fraction of new detections falling outside a previously active matrix is also not surprising by itself, because any increase in detection probability will mechanically generate many boundary gains.

RC8 therefore asks a sharper question:

> **When an environmental pulse enlarges a behaviourally realized community, is the response adequately described as uniform amplification, or does it recruit new spatial and taxonomic participation beyond that expectation?**

This yields three linked questions:

1. **Q1 — Expansion:** does recent-rain activity expand the realized community along both spatial and taxonomic axes?
2. **Q2 — Structure:** is that matrix allocation distinguishable from magnitude-matched uniform activation? This is the central structural test.
3. **Q3 — Differentiation:** does expansion materially erode local pairwise-Sørensen differentiation?

## Main novelty

The strongest contribution is **not** that 92% of incidence growth crosses a matrix boundary. Uniform activation already predicts substantial boundary crossing.

Under the primary frozen null:
- expected boundary crossing = **80.9%**;
- 95% null interval = **74.2–87.4%**;
- observed boundary crossing = **92.0%**;
- four-component omnibus Monte Carlo P = **.000999**.

The null was rejected under κ = 1, 2 and 5 (P = .000999 each).

The deviation is directional:
- corner expansion: **36.9% observed vs 27.4% null mean**;
- spatial spread: **15.2% vs 22.9%**;
- taxonomic deepening: **39.9% vs 30.5%**;
- within-core rearrangement: **8.0% vs 19.1%**.

Thus the observed response is more **taxonomically recruiting** and less **core-rearranging** than uniform amplification predicts.

## General ecological principle

RC8 separates two processes that are often conflated:

### Community amplification
The same underlying participants become more detectable or active.

### Community recruitment
Previously inactive species × site combinations enter the realized community.

Uniform amplification can create substantial apparent expansion on its own. RC8 shows that the observed frog response goes beyond that mechanical expectation.

This supports the broader principle:

> **Environmental pulses can recruit previously inactive spatial and taxonomic participation beyond uniform amplification while preserving local differentiation.**

The result is about the ecology of **realized participation**, not about demographic turnover.

## Why the result is surprising

The null was deliberately advantaged. It was allowed to preserve:
- dry-side species × stop propensities;
- the observed dry matrix;
- the candidate species pool;
- each pair’s wet incidence magnitude in expectation.

Even then, it predicted only about 81% boundary crossing and about 19% within-core rearrangement.

The data instead show 92% boundary crossing and 8% within-core rearrangement.

So the surprising fact is not merely that new rows and columns appear. It is that **new participation enters the matrix more strongly at taxonomic boundaries than a magnitude-matched amplification process predicts**.

## Practical non-homogenization

Pairwise Sørensen has a fixed post hoc ±0.025 practical-equivalence margin.

Primary:
- β = -0.00049;
- 90% CI = **-0.0107 to +0.00968**;
- PASS.

Exact consecutive-year:
- β = +0.00215;
- 90% CI = **-0.00973 to +0.0140**;
- PASS.

This gives Q3 a bounded answer: realized participation can expand without a material collapse of local differentiation.

The observed Sørensen slope itself lies inside the uniform-null Sørensen distribution, so beta diversity is **not** what rejects the uniform-activation null. The structural departure lies in incidence allocation.

## What prior work already owns

RC8 does not claim novelty for:
- rainfall-sensitive frog calling;
- rainfall-associated activity or richness changes;
- fine-temporal acoustic-community composition;
- frog beta diversity;
- inundation-driven richness or composition;
- precipitation effects on alpha/beta/gamma diversity;
- species × site matrices or additive decompositions.

The novelty lies in benchmarking **how** a familiar activation response enters a fixed spatial incidence matrix against an explicit, magnitude-matched amplification null.

## Mechanistic boundary

Activation geometry failed its rain-specific placebo gate and remains demoted.

Uniform-null rejection does not rescue that trait and does not prove heterogeneous activation thresholds. Threshold heterogeneity, inundation and hydric physiology remain discussion-level hypotheses.

## Preferred one-sentence novelty statement

> **A familiar rainfall–calling response expands the realized frog community through more new taxonomic participation and less core rearrangement than uniform amplification predicts, while local Sørensen differentiation remains practically stable.**
