Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Food webs that reproduce frog–predator association signs need not benefit frogs,”** as a Letter in *Ecology Letters*.

Species associations are commonly used to motivate mechanisms of community organization. Yet a food web that reproduces the direction of an association need not improve the abundance of the focal population. Our Letter tests that distinction in a stage-structured frog–odonate–salamander food-web system, and identifies developmental conditions under which vegetation changes from harmful to beneficial.

We organize the evidence in three linked roles. **B (biological premise):** independent INBO and USGS datasets relate adult calling to larval detection within monitored places or visits, while longitudinal Mohonk monitoring documents how breeding stages can be recorded in real vernal pools. These observations do not measure individual juvenile survival or demonstrate a causal predator mechanism. **C (continental observation):** among 4,248 NAAMP listening sites, odonate–frog calling associations differ across caudate and vegetation suitability contexts; the association does not uniquely reveal the natural predator process. **D (controlled mechanism):** a source-audited synthetic food-web screen found 794/20,000 models reproducing four idealized signs. More importantly, two disjoint sets of 256 food-web structures crossed with 27 synthetic rate settings showed that the two vegetation functions increase pattern compatibility by **+2.71/+2.75 percentage points** while decreasing mean simulated frog log-cohort abundance by **−0.040/−0.041**. Separately varying salamander arrival and aquatic-stage duration reversed vegetation's demographic effect, from **−0.186** in the late/short condition to **+0.116** in the early/long condition.

The novelty is the **direct decoupling of association-pattern generation from demographic benefit**, together with a controlled life-history explanation for why vegetation can change the sign of its population effect. Virtual-observer R5 and geographically held-out mechanism-prediction failures are retained in the Supplementary Information as methodological boundaries: current broad acoustic, suitability and landscape data cannot replace controlled counterfactual interventions or identify the wild feeding links. We do not claim that the modeled trophic mechanism has been independently demonstrated in monitored ponds.

We believe this distinction matters across community ecology, ecological networks and stage-dependent habitat effects, where matching a broad spatial signature is often mistakenly treated as an ecological success metric. The report preserves negative and background-sensitive field comparisons, supports the main mechanism outcomes with independent structural replication, and makes direct pond-stage predictions for future tests.
