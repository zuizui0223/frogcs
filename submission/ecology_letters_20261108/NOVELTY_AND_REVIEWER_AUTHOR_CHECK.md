## Novelty emphasis supported after a 2026-11-09 independent primary-literature comparison

- **No B main-result section**: independent INBO and USGS (SI S20/S22) associate calls with recorded larval detection, but do not confirm mating, oviposition, hatching or recruitment; future-year transfer failed. Mohonk is source-described but the proposed 29/30 and 29/59 counts remain unverified. The main empirical result is C, a continental adult-call/environment association, not predator-mediated birth or survival.
- Two independent 256-structure × 27 synthetic-rate food-web sets support a positive gain in pattern generation concurrent with negative average model frog-cohort changes; the early/long predator developmental setting can reverse the effect.
- **R5 is methodological support, not a third major biological finding:** under hypothetical call-link/proxy errors, model mechanisms can be difficult to recover from field-style observations. This explains why the available acoustic/SDM/landscape data cannot substitute for simulated independent manipulation of vegetation functions and predator timing; it does **not** show that such field measurements are inherently impossible, or reveal the reason actual G14/G15 failed.
- **Closest original comparisons** already show tadpole predation by both predator guilds (Caldwell et al. 1980), multiple-predator effects on amphibian survival/metamorphosis (Wilbur & Fauth 1990), indirect frog–newt–dragonfly effects (Kishida et al. 2009; Davenport & Chalcraft 2013), plant structure acting as refuge or hunting perch depending on the predator and prey (Klecka & Boukal 2014; Mocq et al. 2021), and size-mediated priority effects (Rasmussen et al. 2014). Baker et al. 2025 adds a recent test of multichannel salamander cascades. None of those effects should be claimed as first discovered here.
- The C→D manuscript's **two specific source-model contributions** are (i) independent, matched vegetation interventions that make an analogous four-sign pattern more reproducible while reducing mean frog cohorts across 27 hypothetical rate settings and two disjoint legal 256-structure samples, and (ii) a net vegetation effect that changes sign with predator developmental timing and hypothetical frog arrival. No paper in this scoped search was shown to have tested that *entire combination*; this does **not** establish global priority or a natural causal effect.
