## Novelty and field-to-model reviewer defence

**Question:** “Isn't the basic three-species predator system already known?”  
**Evidence:** Yes. Caldwell et al. (1980) and Wilbur & Fauth (1990) demonstrated predator–tadpole relations. **Answer:** This Letter does not claim new edges; it tests the *outcome discrepancy under matched vegetation pathways* and developmental reversal across source-defined models.

**Question:** “Isn't habitat complexity both a refuge and hunting substrate already known?”  
**Evidence:** Yes. Klecka & Boukal (2014), Hossie & Murray (2010), Mocq et al. (2021), Warfe & Barmuta (2004). **Answer:** The two directionally opposed **encoded** habitat pathways are existing ecological ingredients; whether jointly enabling them increases pattern compatibility while decreasing model frog cohorts is the tested result.

**Question:** “Does matching four signs fit the wild frog–predator network?”  
**Answer:** No. Adult calling, habitat suitability and landscape 3-km flooded vegetation are different measurement units from model aquatic cohorts, predator supply and pond refuge; geographically held-out field mechanisms failed under the required two SDM backgrounds. The study uses macroecological **motivation**, not a causal field posterior.

**Question:** “Does the independent validation fully generalize?”  
**Answer:** G11 repeats two predeclared signs over disjoint original model structures and the same 27 artificial rate cells. It supports structural replication *within this model catalogue*, not new independent biological systems or empirical wild parameters.

**Question:** “Are developmental phenology effects novel?”  
**Answer:** Priority and size-mediated shifts are known; the **combined vegetation demographic effect and the model's pattern-versus-benefit reversal** are the distinct source-family predictions. Adults' seasonal choruses do not measure larval hatch dates. Actual overlap requires repeated pond-stage sampling.
