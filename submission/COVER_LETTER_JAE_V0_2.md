# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.3

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Recent rainfall predicts broader frog acoustic participation without stronger residual co-calling associations,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Multispecies detections can increase through distinct ecological and observation processes: more standardized sampling units may become active, a broader species pool may participate across a survey, more species may occur within already-active units, or residual association among species may strengthen. These mechanisms are often conflated when community co-activity is summarized by a single multispecies response.

We use standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys to separate these components and Australian FrogID recordings as an external contrast under different observation conditioning. In NAAMP, recent rainfall predicted more multispecies calling across all surveyed 5-min stops and the rain coefficient remained negative after temperature and nonlinear seasonal adjustment. Decomposition localized that signal to broader participation: recent rain was associated with more acoustically active stops and a larger run-level active species pool, while multispecies calling within already-active stops, independence-residual co-calling, a fixed-marginal shuffle residual, pairwise excess covariance and pairwise network density showed no detectable strengthening.

FrogID reveals an important contrast rather than a direct replication. Every analysed FrogID recording was already acoustically active, and recent rain strongly predicted one-versus-multiple-species recordings. The closest NAAMP activity-conditioned effect was null. We therefore treat this discrepancy explicitly: activity-conditioned multiplicity can differ among systems or observation processes, potentially because of submission behaviour, detectability, exposure definition or community composition.

The general contribution is an inference principle with direct animal-ecology relevance: **multispecies co-activity should be decomposed into participation, observation conditioning and residual association before being interpreted as synchrony, facilitation or temporal-niche reorganization**.

All analyses are observational. Original versioned endpoints are distinguished from reviewer-motivated post-opening diagnostics, and the manuscript reports both the small NAAMP primary effect and the cross-system conditioning discrepancy rather than averaging them away.

Sincerely,

The authors
