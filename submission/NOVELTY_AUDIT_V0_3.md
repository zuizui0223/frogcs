# Novelty audit v0.3 — activation versus residual co-calling

## Revised claim boundary

The manuscript no longer claims that rainfall compresses temporal niches or that conditioning FrogID recordings on at least one caller establishes residual synchrony.

The strongest defensible empirical claim is:

> **Recent rainfall is associated with a greater probability of multispecies frog calling across independent North American and Australian acoustic systems. In standardized NAAMP data, decomposition attributes the rainfall signal to broader acoustic participation rather than detectable strengthening of residual co-calling associations.**

## What is already established

Rainfall effects on frog calling, calling-species richness and chorus onset are established. Hsu et al. (2006), Xie et al. (2017) and Brodie et al. (2025) therefore prevent novelty claims such as “rain makes more frog species call” or “rain synchronizes frog choruses for the first time.”

The statistical/ecological principle that shared environmental responses should be separated from residual multispecies association is also established outside frog bioacoustics. Royan et al. (2016) showed that shared environmental responses can dominate apparent co-occurrence structure, and Swallow et al. (2016) explicitly modelled multispecies synchrony in response to measured covariates separately from unexplained synchrony.

## What the current study adds

1. **Cross-continental event-level replication.** Recent rainfall predicts more one-window multispecies frog detections in standardized NAAMP and independent expert-validated FrogID data.
2. **Standardized acoustic decomposition.** NAAMP separates P(any calling), active species-pool richness, P(>=2 | >=1), pairwise network density, observed-minus-independence co-calling and pairwise excess covariance.
3. **Empirical dissociation.** Rain is associated with acoustic activation and active-pool expansion, while multiple residual-association metrics are null.
4. **Weather robustness.** The NAAMP rain coefficient remains negative after temperature and nonlinear day-of-year adjustment.
5. **Spatial robustness.** The raw rain direction persists within repeated NAAMP routes and within fixed FrogID ERA5 cells.
6. **Inference discipline.** FrogID is treated as independent replication of the raw multispecies event pattern, not as proof of residual synchrony.
7. **Transparent evidence hierarchy.** Original primary/secondary/mechanism contracts predate their effect readbacks; reviewer-motivated independence diagnostics are explicitly labelled post-opening.

## Reviewer-safe general contribution

> A shared environmental pulse can make multispecies activity look more synchronous by broadening participation even when residual co-calling associations do not detectably strengthen.

This is an empirical demonstration in large-scale frog acoustic monitoring, not a claim that the activation-versus-association distinction is itself new.

## Phrases to avoid

- temporal niche compression as a demonstrated result
- rainfall synchronizes frog communities
- conditional FrogID proves an effect beyond independent species activation
- rainfall restructures pairwise interactions
- first evidence that shared environmental responses create apparent synchrony
- post-opening diagnostics were preregistered

## Remaining novelty risk

The paper is closest to the established literature on weather-driven frog calling and to general methods separating shared environmental response from species association. Editorial success therefore depends on foregrounding the **empirical decomposition**, not the raw rain effect or a claim of methodological invention.
