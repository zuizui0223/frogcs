# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.10

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated expansion of frog active communities crosses spatial and taxonomic boundaries without practical homogenization,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling and observed richness are well established. We therefore ask a narrower community-ecological question: **does a short rainfall-associated increase merely amplify baseline acoustic activity, or does it enter the spatial community matrix in a more structured way?**

Using 4,236 wetter–drier comparisons from 585 NAAMP routes in 21 states, we found that rainfall contrast was associated with more active stops, higher richness within active stops and higher route-level active richness. The key advance is an explicit null comparison. A frozen uniform-activation simulation preserved dry-side species × stop propensities and matched each pair’s wetter-run incidence magnitude in expectation. It predicted 80.9% boundary crossing (95% null interval 74.2–87.4%), whereas the observed share was 92.0%. The full four-component allocation rejected uniform activation under all three pre-frozen smoothing specifications (Monte Carlo P = .001 each): corner expansion and taxonomic deepening were overrepresented, while within-core rearrangement was 8.0% observed versus 19.1% expected.

Pairwise Sørensen differentiation was separately bounded by a fixed post hoc practical-equivalence margin of ±0.025 slope units. The primary 90% CI (-0.0107 to +0.00968) and exact-consecutive-year CI (-0.00973 to +0.0140) both lay within that margin. We therefore use “without practical homogenization” only for pairwise Sørensen within this bound.

The headline pattern was robust to recorded hearing/noise/wind conditions, omission of any single state, and exclusion of comparisons entirely inside the programme’s 0–3 day rain-target window. Rainfall timing was observational, so we do not infer causality. Species-level mechanism also remains unresolved: a proposed activation-geometry trait failed a frozen placebo gate and was removed from the headline rather than retuned.

We believe the manuscript fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it moves beyond another demonstration that rain changes frog activity or richness. It shows that a familiar behavioural response expands the behaviourally realized community in a way that a magnitude-matched uniform activation process cannot reproduce, while local Sørensen differentiation remains practically stable.

Sincerely,

The authors
