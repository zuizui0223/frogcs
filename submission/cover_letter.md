# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation is concentrated within taxa across multiple sites,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall-sensitive calling is familiar in anurans. We ask a different ecological question: when a short favourable pulse activates calling across a landscape, is the realised multi-site pattern fully explained by species-specific rainfall responses, prior site use and the total amount of activation, or does additional within-taxon spatial structure remain?

We analysed 4,236 matched wetter–drier comparisons from 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys across 585 routes and 21 U.S. states. The focal test used 2,916 comparisons with strictly prior physical-site history. A route-cross-fit comparator representing taxon-specific rainfall sensitivity, prior species × SiteID use, dry-state persistence and total wet incidence predicted a within-taxon concentration coefficient of **1.353**, versus **1.650 observed**. The conditional residual was **0.297**, outside the simulated 95% range of −0.132 to 0.119 (P ≈ 0.001, the minimum plus-one value from 1,000 simulations). Strong wet-state chorusing also preferentially reappeared at historically strong physical sites (β = 0.151), while the excess was concentrated in a deeper multi-site tail.

We believe the paper fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it moves beyond another mean rainfall response to ask how a behavioural pulse is spatially organized after response magnitude is represented. The observed pattern falls between independent wetland reactions and a uniform route-wide switch: activity remains over-concentrated within taxa and selectively expressed at recurrent taxon-specific chorus locations. This motivates a broader testable hypothesis that response magnitude alone may be insufficient to describe a short behavioural pulse when the realised spatial pattern retains additional ecological structure.

The integrated framing was developed after the initial NAAMP analyses were inspected, and we state this explicitly. Cross-fitting prevents focal-route leakage but is not independent confirmation; the manuscript is presented as a geographically broad exploratory discovery with explicit internal falsification. Transferability beyond NAAMP remains untested. A concrete monitoring implication is that treating route stops as independent can materially understate uncertainty: pair-clustered SE exceeded IID values in 38/39 eligible taxa (median 1.61-fold), while route clustering increased the pooled rainfall-effect SE 2.69-fold.

The study is observational and does not infer rainfall causality, literal synchrony among sequentially surveyed stops, individual movement, demographic occupancy change or a unique lower-level mechanism. The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review and is accompanied by Supporting Information, reproducible figures and an auditable analysis history.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

The authors
