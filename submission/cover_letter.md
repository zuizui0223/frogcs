# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation reveals recurrent within-taxon multi-site organization,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall-sensitive calling is familiar in anurans. We ask a different question: **when a short environmental pulse recruits taxa into an active chorus landscape, is the resulting multi-site pattern simply the sum of species-specific responses and historically suitable sites, or does activity retain additional dependence across sites within the same taxa?**

We analysed 4,236 matched wetter–drier comparisons from 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys across 585 routes and 21 U.S. states. Rain-associated change often involved direct transitions from acoustic silence into overlapping or full chorus. The unusual spatial component emerged at third-and-later occupied sites within recruited taxa.

Our principal test used 2,916 matched pairs with strictly prior physical-site history. A route-cross-fit comparator allowed taxa to differ in rainfall sensitivity, represented prior species × SiteID use, retained dry-state persistence and matched total wet incidence. It predicted a within-taxon concentration coefficient of **1.353 versus 1.650 observed**; the conditional residual was **0.297**, beyond the simulated 95% range of −0.132 to 0.119 (P = 0.000999). A stronger held-out rain × history gate still underpredicted the observed concentration. Strong wet-state chorusing also preferentially reappeared at physical sites where the same taxon had chorused strongly before (β = 0.151), with that historical-site targeting strengthened toward the survey closer to rain (β = 0.0245).

We believe this fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because the result concerns not another mean rainfall response, but a **pulse-revealed dependence structure in joint species × place activity**. The wet-state chorus is more spatially concentrated within taxa than first-order species responses, static site propensities and total activation magnitude predict. This provides a testable broader hypothesis for behaviourally cryptic communities: short environmental pulses may expose recurrent spatial organization that is weakly expressed during inactive periods.

The integrated hypothesis was developed post-opening within NAAMP, and we state this explicitly. We have therefore treated prospective confirmation as part of the evidentiary design rather than as a future aspiration. **Before access to any Wisconsin Frog and Toad Survey species × station × year outcomes, we froze the confirmatory authority on 1 October 2026 (commit `1aba268e8317`; analysis specification v0.5: `4ef112ca4253`).** Wisconsin is not among the 21 NAAMP discovery states, so the external test does not reuse a discovery-state route. Its outcome interpretation is also frozen in advance: the same endpoint/comparator yields either replication support, an informative non-replication only when uncertainty excludes an effect at least half the NAAMP discovery residual, or an inconclusive non-PASS when precision is insufficient. No WFTS frog-response outcome was inspected to set these rules.

The study remains observational and does not infer literal synchrony among sequentially surveyed stops, individual movement, demographic occupancy change or a unique lower-level mechanism. The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review, is within the journal's word limits, and is accompanied by Supporting Information, reproducible figures and an auditable analysis history.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

The authors
