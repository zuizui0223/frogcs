# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.5

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Recent rainfall predicts week-long richness elevation and species-selective reassembly in active frog communities,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling are well known. We therefore ask a different ecological question: does a rainfall pulse merely produce an immediate chorus response, or does it shift the richness and membership of the active community over subsequent days?

Using 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys, we find that active-community richness remains elevated across prespecified rain-recency classes through **4–7 days** after reported rain. Relative to surveys ≥8 days since rain, adjusted richness was +0.654 species on day 0, +0.544 on day 1, +0.411 on days 2–3 and +0.381 on days 4–7. A stricter exact-consecutive-year matched sensitivity clearly supports the richness signal through days 2–3 and remains directionally positive but less precise at days 4–7.

The response is also compositionally structured. Across 4,236 matched wetter–drier route comparisons, rainfall contrast predicted richness gain, the wetter assemblage was not a simple nested extension of the drier assemblage, and turnover increased in the exact consecutive-year sensitivity. Twenty-nine species showed extreme wet-versus-dry heterogeneity after environmental adjustment, and more than half of heterogeneity among family-matched species remained within families.

We therefore interpret the result as a **multi-day expansion and species-selective reassembly of the acoustically active community**, rather than the familiar observation that frogs simply call during rain. The manuscript does not infer occupancy change, colonization, extinction, breeding success, rainfall causality or a physiological response half-life. Follow-up tests of simple trait and environmental explanations did not identify a robust mechanism, which we report as a boundary rather than promoting a post hoc story.

We believe the study fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it shows that a short environmental pulse can alter the behaviourally realized richness and composition of an animal community for several days, within the same sites and seasonal sampling windows.

Sincerely,

The authors
