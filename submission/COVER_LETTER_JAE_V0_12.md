# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology v0.12

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated expansion of frog active communities crosses spatial and taxonomic boundaries without practical homogenization,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling and observed richness are already well established. We therefore ask a broader question: **when a short environmental pulse enlarges a behaviourally realized community, does it simply amplify the same participants, or does it recruit additional spatial and taxonomic participation?**

Using 4,236 wetter–drier comparisons from 585 standardized NAAMP routes in 21 U.S. states, we found expansion along both spatial and taxonomic axes. The key structural test compared the observed species × stop allocation with a frozen magnitude-matched uniform-activation null. That null already predicted 80.9% boundary crossing (95% interval 74.2–87.4%), but the observed share was 92.0%. The full four-component allocation rejected uniform activation under all three frozen smoothing specifications (Monte Carlo P = .001 each): corner expansion and taxonomic deepening were overrepresented, while within-core rearrangement was 8.0% observed versus 19.1% expected.

We then externally tested the most transportable component—taxonomic depth within an already-active acoustic unit—in 40,754 expert-validated Australian FrogID recordings. Each 1-SD increase in antecedent dry-spell exposure reduced excess richness by 0.0818 species (95% CI -0.0982 to -0.0654), with recorder-clustered and within-ERA5-cell sensitivities agreeing. Thus rainfall-associated taxonomic deepening was directionally consistent across independent North American and Australian monitoring systems, while the detailed spatial geometry remained identifiable only in the fixed-route NAAMP design.

Pairwise Sørensen differentiation in NAAMP was separately practically equivalent to zero change within a fixed post hoc ±0.025 slope margin. The headline pattern was also robust to recorded hearing/noise/wind conditions, omission of any single state and exclusion of comparisons confined to the programme’s 0–3-day rain-target window.

We do not infer rainfall causality, demographic recruitment or worldwide universality. A proposed species-level activation-geometry trait failed a frozen placebo gate and remains demoted.

We believe the manuscript fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it distinguishes **community amplification** from **community recruitment** and shows that one component of recruitment—taxonomic deepening within already-active units—is cross-continentally consistent, while standardized spatial data reveal additional boundary-biased expansion without practical homogenization.

Sincerely,

The authors
