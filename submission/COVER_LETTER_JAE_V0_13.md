# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated expansion of frog active communities is more boundary-biased than uniform activation predicts,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall effects on frog calling and observed richness are well established. We therefore ask a community-ecological question: **when a short environmental pulse enlarges a behaviourally realized community, where does the added participation enter a fixed species × site matrix, and can that allocation be explained by uniform activation?**

Using 4,236 wetter–drier comparisons from 585 standardized NAAMP routes in 21 U.S. states, we found expansion along both spatial and taxonomic axes. A magnitude-matched uniform-activation null already predicted substantial boundary crossing (80.9%, 95% interval 74.2–87.4%), but the observed share was 92.0%; the full four-component allocation rejected the null under all three frozen smoothing specifications (Monte Carlo P = .001 each).

We then addressed a key robustness concern directly. A separately frozen **persistence-preserving stress test** removed hierarchical species-level shrinkage and strongly anchored each pair to its observed dry species × stop state before applying the common activation shift. Under the primary 75% anchor, expected boundary crossing was 77.9% (95% interval 73.4–82.8%), within-core rearrangement was 22.1% expected versus 8.0% observed, and the four-component omnibus again gave P = .001. The result remained P = .001 even with 90% dry-state anchoring. Thus the allocation departure is not explained by a null model that makes persistent species × stop identities too exchangeable.

We also aligned the active-unit taxonomic-depth estimand with 40,754 Australian FrogID recordings. Because earlier FrogID analyses had already shown a rain-associated multispecies signal, we treat this as **cross-dataset directional consistency**, not independent validation. Taxonomic depth had the same recent-rain direction in NAAMP and FrogID, including recorder-clustered and within-weather-cell Australian sensitivities.

Pairwise Sørensen change was small and its confidence interval fell within a post hoc practical bound, but the observed slope was also ordinary under uniform activation. We therefore retain compositional stability only as secondary context rather than a title-level discovery.

We believe the manuscript fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it distinguishes **community amplification** from **community recruitment**: recent-rain expansion enters the realized community more strongly across spatial and taxonomic boundaries than uniform activation predicts, even under a persistence-favouring null.

Sincerely,

The authors
