# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation shows higher-order spatial coherence and species-specific site recurrence,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall-sensitive calling is one of the most familiar features of anuran reproductive ecology. Our study asks a different question: **when recent-rain conditions recruit taxa into an active chorus landscape, is the resulting multi-site pattern simply the sum of more taxa and more occupied sites, or does the behavioural pulse reveal higher-order spatial organization?**

We analysed 4,236 matched wetter–drier comparisons from 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys across 585 routes and 21 U.S. states. Much of the rainfall-associated CallingIndex change arose from previously silent species × site cells becoming active, and most of that activation entered directly at overlapping or full-chorus states. The unusual spatial response emerged after recruited taxa reached third-and-later sites, where 97.3% of the rainfall coefficient was carried by CallingIndex 2–3.

The central result is a higher-order allocation effect. For every matched pair and direction, we conditioned exactly on both the number of newly recruited taxa and their total species × site incidence. Even with those first-order quantities fixed, recent-rain advantage predicted excess concentration of activity within the same taxa (β = 0.244, 95% CI 0.147–0.340). The effect remained in 3,115 comparisons with the same observer at the same physical sites (β = 0.254, 0.138–0.371), was distributed across taxa and states, and was not reproduced by route-cross-fitted species-specific rainfall responses, strictly prior species × physical-site history, dry-state persistence, or a held-out rain × history gate.

The spatial coherence was also historically placed rather than arbitrary. Within the same matched pair and taxon, physical sites with prior strong chorusing were more likely to host later wet-state strong chorusing (β = 0.151, 95% CI 0.129–0.173), and proximity to rain selectively strengthened full-chorus placement at historically strong sites (β = 0.0245, 0.0070–0.0420).

We believe the study fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses a familiar behavioural response to expose a broader ecological principle: **short environmental pulses can reveal higher-order spatial organization on recurrent species × place templates in behaviourally cryptic communities.** The paper remains explicitly an observational study of reproductive acoustic activity; it does not infer literal synchrony among sequentially surveyed stops, individual movement, demographic occupancy change, or a unique lower-level mechanism.

The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review. The current main document is within the journal's 8,500-word Research Article limit, uses a numbered abstract below 350 words, and is accompanied by Supporting Information and a reproducible five-figure set. Data and analysis provenance are documented in the public repository, with a persistent archive DOI to be added at final archiving.

Thank you for your consideration.
