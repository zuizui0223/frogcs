# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Rainfall-associated frog chorus activation shows within-taxon multi-site coherence and species-specific site recurrence,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Rainfall-sensitive calling is one of the most familiar features of anuran reproductive ecology. We ask a different community-level question: **when recent-rain conditions recruit taxa into an active chorus landscape, is the multi-site pattern reproduced by species-specific rainfall responses and historically used sites, or does extra spatial participation remain unusually concentrated within the same taxa?**

We analysed 4,236 matched wetter–drier comparisons from 15 years of standardized North American Amphibian Monitoring Program surveys across 585 routes and 21 U.S. states. Much of the rainfall-associated CallingIndex change arose from previously silent species × site cells becoming active, and most of that activation entered directly at overlapping or full-chorus states. The unusual spatial component emerged after recruited taxa reached third-and-later sites, where 97.3% of the rainfall coefficient was carried by CallingIndex 2–3.

Our principal spatial comparison uses 2,916 matched pairs with strictly prior physical-site history. A route-cross-fit model allowed taxa to differ in rainfall sensitivity, represented prior species × SiteID use, retained dry-state persistence and matched the observed total wet incidence. It predicted a within-taxon concentration coefficient of 1.353 versus 1.650 observed; the conditional residual was 0.297, beyond the simulated 95% residual interval (−0.132 to 0.119; P = 0.000999). Adding a held-out rain × history gate still underpredicted the observed concentration (1.332; P = 0.000999). Thus the result is not simply that recent rain recruits taxa that are generally more widespread or historically common across route stops.

The coherent response was also historically placed. Within the same matched pair and taxon, physical sites with prior strong chorusing were more likely to host later wet-state strong chorusing (β = 0.151, 95% CI 0.129–0.173), and proximity to rain selectively strengthened full-chorus placement at historically strong sites (β = 0.0245, 0.0070–0.0420).

We believe the study fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses a familiar behavioural response to expose a broader ecological principle: **short environmental pulses can reveal non-independent multi-site organization on recurrent species × place templates in behaviourally cryptic communities.** We deliberately avoid “higher-order interactions” terminology; the result concerns within-taxon spatial concentration, not multi-species interaction terms.

The integrated framing is explicitly post-opening and exploratory within NAAMP. Cross-fitting prevents route leakage in fitted comparator terms but does not provide independent confirmation, and the manuscript states that prospective external replication is the next decisive test. The paper also remains observational: it does not infer literal synchrony among sequentially surveyed stops, individual movement, demographic occupancy change or a unique lower-level mechanism.

The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review, is within the journal's word limits, and is accompanied by Supporting Information and a reproducible five-figure set.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

The authors
