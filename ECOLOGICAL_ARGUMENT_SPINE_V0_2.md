# Ecological argument spine v0.3 — persistent species rain response predicts reassembly

## Working title

**Species-specific rainfall sensitivity predicts short-term reassembly of active frog communities**

## Biological question

Rainfall-driven frog calling is established. The ecological question is now more specific:

> **Are short-term changes in frog community membership predictable from persistent species differences in sensitivity to recent rain?**

The paper is about community dynamics generated by repeatable species-level environmental response, not detection methodology.

## Claim 1 — wetter matched runs contain richer active communities

Across 4,236 same-route, same-seasonal-window wetter–drier pairs:

- β richness gain = **+0.2859 species / unit log-rain contrast**
- 95% CI **0.1646–0.4072**
- P = **3.81 × 10^-6**

Exact consecutive-year sensitivity:

- β = **+0.3009**
- P = **1.60 × 10^-4**

## Claim 2 — richness gain includes compositional replacement

Nestedness does not increase:

- primary P = **0.279**
- consecutive-year P = **0.711**

Turnover:

- primary β = +0.01264, P = 0.104
- exact consecutive-year β = **+0.01840**
- 95% CI **0.00470–0.03211**
- P = **0.00850**

Thus wet assemblages are not merely dry assemblages plus extra species.

## Claim 3 — the response is species-selective

Adjusted wet-versus-dry species responses:

- 29/29 estimable
- Q = **144.01**, df = 28
- P = **1.46 × 10^-17**
- raw vs adjusted Spearman ρ = **0.997**

Family structure does not absorb the heterogeneity:

- within-family Q = **73.88**, df = 21
- P = **8.28 × 10^-8**
- 52.1% of total family-matched Q remains within families

## Claim 4 — species response is predictable across non-overlapping years

Training period:

- **2001–2007**
- 2,888 eligible runs
- per-species within-route rain-recency sensitivity
- 27/29 species estimable

Validation period:

- **2008–2015**
- 4,960 eligible runs
- 2,459 matched wet–dry pairs
- 29/29 species estimable
- 27 species overlap with training estimates

Primary cross-period result:

- β later wet-recruitment per 1 SD greater early rain sensitivity = **+0.2844**
- 95% CI **0.1407–0.4281**
- P = **1.05 × 10^-4**
- Spearman ρ = **0.458**
- P = **0.0162**

Family-stratified permutation:

- 24 species / 5 families
- within-family slope = **+0.1933**
- positive-tail P = **0.0156**
- two-sided P = **0.0344**

Therefore:

> **Species-specific rainfall sensitivity is persistent enough to forecast later community reassembly.**

This is the main mechanistic result.

## What the mechanism result means

Use:

- persistent species-specific rain response;
- behavioral rain-pulse sensitivity;
- temporally predictive environmental response;
- predictable species-selective reassembly.

Do not claim:

- physiology was measured;
- rain sensitivity is genetically fixed;
- rainfall causes colonization or extinction;
- the time split makes the analysis causal.

## Negative mechanism programme

The final mechanism result was reached after a finite sequence of frozen tests.

Not supported / not estimable:

- body size as a family-robust mechanism;
- collapsed hydroperiod axis;
- breeding-season breadth;
- route-local core/satellite recruitment.

These negatives strengthen the interpretation that the predictive axis is **species-specific rain response itself**, while its lower-level physiological or life-history basis remains unresolved.

## General ecological contribution

> **Behaviourally realized communities can be predictably reorganized by short environmental pulses because constituent species differ persistently in their sensitivity to the same cue.**

This is stronger than “rainfall changes frog activity” and stronger than an after-the-fact species list: the response ranking measured in earlier years predicts later community membership.

## Hard boundaries

- acoustically active community, not occupancy community;
- association, not rainfall causality;
- wet recruitment means acoustic participation, not colonization;
- dry loss means absence from that survey, not extinction;
- no body-size, hydroperiod or breeding-season mechanism claim;
- no further post-opening mechanism hunting without new external data or reviewer/editor request.
