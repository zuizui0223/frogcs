# RC7 causal and mechanistic triangulation v0.1

## Purpose

This document separates what frogcs directly establishes from what independent anuran literature makes biologically plausible. It does not authorize a causal rainfall claim or a new species-level response trait.

## Layer 1 — directly supported by frogcs

The NAAMP matched analysis establishes an **observational association** between rainfall recency contrast and the behaviourally realized acoustic community:

- active spatial footprint increases;
- local alpha richness increases;
- route gamma richness increases;
- measured among-active-site beta diversity shows no detectable shift;
- active-matrix fill is practically equivalent within the frozen margin;
- about 92% of rainfall-associated species × stop incidence growth crosses a spatial and/or taxonomic matrix boundary;
- the three headline slopes remain confidence-interval positive under every single-state omission.

These results concern acoustic participation, not abundance, occupancy, colonization, extinction or reproductive success.

## Layer 2 — observation-process interpretation

Calling-survey observations are known to be behaviour-sensitive rather than direct population censuses. Royle & Link (2005) developed a latent-class framework specifically for NAAMP calling indices because observed calling intensity is affected by calling behaviour and imperfect detection.

The NAAMP national protocol also conditioned survey timing on rainfall in some regions: Gulf Coast and Great Plains programmes documented the last rainfall event because routes were intended to be surveyed within three days of rain, based on regional calling behaviour. Therefore DaysSinceRain is partly a weather variable and partly a programme-scheduling variable in those regions.

**Implication:** this strengthens the biological interpretation that recent rain matters for acoustic realization, but weakens any attempt to read the association causally. The appropriate estimand is the weather-associated realized acoustic community under NAAMP sampling, not a randomized rainfall treatment effect.

## Layer 3 — external evidence for heterogeneous activation

### Species-specific meteorological response

Oseen & Wassersug (2002) measured five sympatric anurans and found that each species' calling activity was associated with a distinct combination of meteorological variables.

**Supports:** species-specific activation thresholds/response surfaces.

**Does not establish:** the thresholds governing frogcs species or routes.

### Multiple breeding strategies within one regional fauna

Saenz et al. (2006) monitored eight ponds in eastern Texas nightly for two years and described multiple calling/breeding strategies, including rainfall-dependent seasonal breeding and flood-rainfall-dependent opportunistic breeding.

**Supports:** rainfall can recruit different species into calling activity through different breeding strategies even within one region.

### Different rainfall windows and thresholds

Brodie et al. (2025) used continuous acoustic monitoring of a tropical savanna assemblage. Explosive breeders often commenced chorusing on the night of or immediately after high rainfall, while prolonged breeders responded to longer rainfall windows and other environmental cues. Species differed in rainfall thresholds and lags.

**Supports:** heterogeneous temporal thresholds and lags can turn a common rainfall pulse into non-uniform community recruitment.

### Habitat/inundation pathway

Sarker et al. (2022) found that wetland inundation increased frog richness and that chorusing responses differed among species and sites.

**Supports:** rainfall-associated hydrological change can alter both habitat availability and acoustic participation without requiring a uniform community response.

### Hydration pathway

Field measurements show that frog hydration state differs between wet and dry seasons and among ecological habits (Tracy et al., 2014). Anuran water exchange is strongly controlled through specialized cutaneous uptake pathways (Lemenager, Tracy & Christian, 2022).

**Supports:** moisture/hydration is a physiologically plausible route by which rainfall conditions could change behavioural activity.

**Does not establish:** that hydration mediates the frogcs rainfall association.

## Layer 4 — synthesis hypothesis

The most coherent mechanism hypothesis is **heterogeneous recruitment across activation thresholds**:

1. rainfall changes immediate moisture conditions and, in some systems, breeding-site inundation;
2. species and sites differ in calling, movement and breeding thresholds and in response lags;
3. a common rainfall pulse therefore recruits different latent participants at different sites;
4. active spatial footprint and taxonomic depth expand simultaneously;
5. because recruitment is heterogeneous rather than uniform, local communities need not converge and beta structure need not collapse.

This hypothesis is consistent with:
- frogcs boundary-crossing expansion;
- frogcs state-level heterogeneity;
- Oseen & Wassersug's species-specific weather responses;
- Saenz et al.'s multiple breeding strategies;
- Brodie et al.'s threshold/lag differences;
- Sarker et al.'s species- and site-specific inundation response.

It remains a **Discussion hypothesis**, not a mechanism identified by frogcs.

## Causal language boundary

Authorized:
- "biologically concordant with"
- "consistent with"
- "plausible pathway"
- "supports an activation-threshold interpretation"
- "independent literature provides mechanistic plausibility"

Not authorized:
- "rainfall caused the expansion"
- "hydration mediated the expansion"
- "inundation mediated the expansion"
- "activation thresholds were identified in NAAMP"
- "the same mechanism operates in every state"
