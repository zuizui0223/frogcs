# NAAMP species composition and Figure 1
Exploratory sensitivity on the CD Letter cohort, 2001–2015. Files were produced from the SHA256-pinned Counts.csv and Stops.csv in the [USGS release](https://doi.org/10.5066/F7G44NG0). Run research/scripts/naamp_species_strong_call_sensitivity.py RAW_DIR OUTPUT_DIR from the repository root. Only aggregated results are published.

## Reconstruction
Every original site's proportion of completed visits with any retained taxon calling at index ≥2 was reconstructed, including simultaneous choruses. The all-label reconstruction matched all 63,180 visit indicators, 36,554 strong visits and all 4,248 site rates. Index ≥1 detections and index ≥2 events are different rankings. Frequency cutoffs refer to individual species labels, retaining complex labels unless indicated. Species classified as absent from a visit are recorded non-detections.

## Comparison
The Figure 1 source analysis ranks the continuous 3-km flooded vegetation mean, suitability and response, then fits all two-way terms and the three-way term. The 0/positive vegetation coding in other field-model comparisons is a separate specification. OS is odonate × pond caudate suitability at centered vegetation; OV is odonate suitability × vegetation at centered caudate suitability. The source coefficients and 95% limits reproduced for both terms, both backgrounds and both spatial comparisons. The 1,000 paired route resamples rerank all variables, condition on SDMs and retain the observed choice of top taxa. Column delta_vs_all is the paired change on the restandardized rank scale, not an additive share of the total coefficient.

interaction_results.csv contains the full union, pooled removals (top 1, 3, 5 and 10 by all calls and separately by strong calls), removals including ambiguous complexes for the top five, 53 individual-label outcomes, and 53 single-label omissions. species_support.csv gives detection effort and strong detections that were unique to a label. detected_route_sensitivity.csv restricts each of the ten most frequently detected species to whole routes where it was recorded at any intensity, retaining all surveyed sites on those routes. These point sensitivities describe geography, not independently estimated ranges or detection-adjusted occupancy.

## Findings
Pseudacris crucifer supplied the only strong call at 13,072 visits (35.8% of strong visits). Without it, whole-region OV remained negative under both SDMs (−0.071 and −0.092), including both pointwise 95% intervals. OS changed from −0.086 to +0.070 with target-group suitability (paired change +0.156, interval +0.120 to +0.193) and remained negative with uniform suitability.

Removing the top five by any-call frequency left 16,082 strong visits. Whole-region OV was −0.031 and −0.081, with the uniform interval including zero. Both within-route OV intervals included zero (point estimates −0.013 and −0.008). Whole-region OS was −0.120 and +0.117, while within-route OS was −0.010 and −0.028. Ranking the top five by strong calling changed the uniform OV result to −0.062 [−0.094, −0.023]. Removing ten common species further weakened the uniform OV estimate. These differences identify composition sensitivity on the original geographic and rank scales.

Species-specific examples: whole-region OS was negative for P. crucifer (−0.158, −0.346) and positive for Acris gryllus (+0.111, +0.146); A. gryllus had negative OV (−0.058, −0.110). All three comparisons had pointwise intervals excluding zero for both backgrounds. Within-route OS estimates for both species were much closer to zero. Lithobates clamitans and Anaxyrus americanus retained negative OV point estimates within their respective detected routes under both SDMs. Species intervals are exploratory, unadjusted for multiplicity. Sparse taxa, differences in geographic distributions, seasonal coverage and detectability limit ecological interpretation; these analyses do not establish species-specific habitat preference or trophic mechanisms.
