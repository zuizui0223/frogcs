# Shared-site test of the Pseudacris crucifer and Acris gryllus contrast

Post-hoc comparison on the 2001–2015 NAAMP cohort. Raw Counts.csv and Stops.csv match the published SHA256 pins. Use python research/scripts/naamp_shared_site_pair.py RAW_DIR OUTPUT_DIR. Exact named index 1–3 detections defined eligibility; complex records were not assigned to either species. The outcome is the proportion of completed visits with that species at index ≥2. Each species has the same denominator on each site; suitability and ranked 3-km vegetation follow Fig. 1. The contrast is the difference between separately rank-standardized slopes (A minus P), not a difference in absolute calling probability. Both species were kept paired in all 1,000 route bootstrap draws. Intervals are pointwise and conditional on the observed site selection and fitted SDMs.

Of 4,248 sites, 317 (59 routes; 126 positive for flooded vegetation) had both species recorded at some time, providing 4,982 visits; both called on 75 visits. Under the two SDM backgrounds the pooled OS differences were +0.062 [−0.302,+0.372] and +0.188 [−0.291,+0.680]. In the narrower cohort of 565 site-years with both species detected (260 sites, 54 routes, 1,722 visits), the point slopes were opposite under both backgrounds, but the paired intervals still contained zero. Within-route results and vegetation interactions are in paired_coefficients.csv.

Phenology uses the median observed calling day of each species within each shared site-year. Subtract within site-year, average years within site and average sites, resampling entire routes. A. gryllus was later on average by 77.9 days [71.4,83.8] at index ≥1, and by 84.6 days [78.4,91.0] in the 355 site-years with both strong. These are visit-conditioned dates of adult calling; monthly.csv reports denominators, including silent visits. Sparse survey dates constrain temporal resolution.

For the same 317 sites a second response subtracts the month-specific strong-call fraction estimated from all other routes in this selected cohort, before aggregation to site. It assesses sampling calendar sensitivity; its intervals condition on these reference rates. A separate calendar diagnostic uses only site-years with visits in both March–April and May–June, weighting bands equally within year and years equally within site (1,104 site-years, 315 sites). The direct species contrasts include zero in both diagnostics. Sites are selected by detection, and ranges, regional abundance, survey conditions and detectability may still differ. Selection on detection of both species in the same year is an additional sensitivity only.

The substantial adult timing difference survives exact site-year matching. The guild suitability predictor has no information on caudate arrival, size or aquatic duration in these seasons; frog larval duration and cohort survival were not observed. Neither this contrast nor calendar correction partitions the causal contributions of geography and developmental overlap.
