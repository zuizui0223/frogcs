# Frog larval arrival and the two functions of vegetation

Exploratory controlled extension of P1. The 77.9-day matched-site difference in adult calling suggested a timing sensitivity. Frog larval arrival was varied across counterfactual single-frog-guild runs; neither lag to hatching nor the two species' vital rates were estimated. The two frog types do not compete in the same run.

All 256 P1 structural backgrounds, 4 vegetation arms, 4 salamander settings, 8 supply/vegetation vertices and original attack/recruitment settings were retained. Early/late salamanders arrive day 0/40, short/long aquatic stage 40/60 days. Baseline frog and odonate arrivals day 20, frog duration 45, odonate duration 60 (some source structures delay emergence up to 5 days). Ten timing calendars × 4,096 scenarios each, 80 years at half-day intervals, average over the last 20. Seven frog delays 0/20/40/60/71/78/84 days with both predator calendars fixed, plus three calendar shifts at frog delay 78: shift salamander only, odonate only, or both by 78 days. For primary contrasts all calendars have water to day 180. Additional 4,096 scenarios use the original drying day 120 at frog delay 78 and fixed predators. This intervention removes the opportunity for local frog recruitment irrespective of predator conditions.

Net effect = both vegetation functions minus neither, on mean log aquatic frog cohort, averaged across eight source vertices, then equally across food-web backgrounds. Shelter = shelter alone minus neither; feeding support = support alone minus neither; residual = net minus those two terms. These compare the specified functions at unchanged environmental settings. Cumulative hazards H_SF and H_DF are reported separately at the high-vegetation/high-predator vertex. The 1,000 paired structure-bootstrap intervals describe variation across the model family. Source-level summaries, direct paired changes from the early baseline, numerical checks and graph are adjacent to this report.

With fixed predator seasons, a 40-day frog delay switches the early/long salamander net effect from +0.1163 to −0.0452, and the late/long effect from −0.1860 to +0.0422 (structure interval +0.0085 to +0.0785). During frog days 60–105, late/long salamanders overlap for 40 days, late/short for only 20 and early/long for zero. Within the late/long setting, +0.1666 from refuge, −0.1174 from feeding support and −0.0069 from nonadditivity explain the sign. At +78 days, tadpoles arrive on day 98, after the original odonate aquatic stage and with at most 2 days of salamander overlap. In the early/long regime at this frog delay, moving the salamander calendar alone gives +0.3797, moving the odonate calendar alone gives −0.2866, and moving both reproduces +0.1163. Only relative timing changes biological overlap when seasons are otherwise identical. These comparisons do not imply actual regional predator phenologies or species-specific survival.

Reproduction: python research/scripts/frog_arrival_p1.py (Python, NumPy, pandas, SciPy and numba), then python research/scripts/plot_frog_arrival_p1.py. The compiled equivalent of P1 reproduced all 32,768 original vertices for F_B and both frog hazards (maximum error 2.31e−14). The original Python seasonal_original routine was also run at frog delay 40 for all four source-0 late/long arms, matching mean log cohort and both hazards to 6.81e−16. Joint calendar translation matched the baseline within 1e−12. Results depend on the fixed attacked stages, aquatic durations and recruitment parameters; no calibration of adult calls to larval emergence is assumed.
