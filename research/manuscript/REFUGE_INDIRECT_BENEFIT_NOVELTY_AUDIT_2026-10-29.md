# Refuge-mediated loss of indirect predator benefits: primary-source novelty audit

Date: 2026-10-29. Targeted literature audit; not a systematic review or a priority claim. Companion notes: `NOVELTY_MECHANISM_PRIMARY_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md` and `AMPLIFICATION_BOUNDARY_PRIMARY_AUDIT_2026-10-29.md`.

## Verdict

**Habitat structure changing predator antagonism, and thereby changing the balance between direct harm and indirect benefits to shared prey, is established ecology.** Even vegetation increasing predation on amphibians through protection of predators has an experimental precedent. The inspected studies did not directly establish the exact proposed sequence: vegetation selectively protects shared prey F from intermediate predator S, thereby reducing the benefit of top predator D consuming S, so D's net marginal effect on F becomes more harmful. That narrower distinction is a candidate mechanism comparison, not evidence of a new ecological principle or a defensible “first.”

The useful contribution would be to test **which protected trophic link produces a particular combination of marginal effects**, distinguish it from other habitat-mediated pathways, and assess compatibility with independent broad-scale calling patterns. Pattern compatibility alone cannot establish the protected link in real ponds. Adult calls, landscape flooded vegetation and predator suitability do not directly measure larval survival, within-pond refuge structure or predator consumption.

## Scope and verification

Searches combined `intraguild predation`, `habitat complexity`, `refuge`, `shared prey`, `indirect benefit`, `trophic cascade`, `newt`, `dragonfly`, `salamander` and `size structure`; searches also followed named papers and references in accessible primary articles. Nine original studies were retained below. Publisher full text or primary institutional records were inspected where available; abstract-only evidence is explicitly marked. One synthesis is included separately as background. Search snippets from secondary aggregators were not used as sole biological evidence. Some PMC opens returned a browser challenge; accessible publisher or institutional copies were used instead.

This audit is directed toward exact pathway identity, not an exhaustive bibliography. “No exact precedent found” means only within this inspected set.

Its meta-analysis found weaker IGP effects on IG prey in structured habitats but no significant corresponding habitat moderation of the effect on shared prey. This rules out treating refuge structure as a universally beneficial or universally harmful modifier.

## Exact edge distinction and a mechanistic sign check

Use roles rather than permanent species labels: F = shared frog prey at a susceptible stage; S = intermediate predator; D = top predator during the relevant size window. D and S can exchange roles as sizes change. Distinguish these hypotheses:

1. **F protected from S:** V lowers S→F consumption at fixed densities. D removing S then prevents fewer F deaths; D's own F consumption is less offset. This is the focal candidate.
2. **S protected from D:** V lowers D→S consumption. More S survives to consume F; indirect relief is lost through reduced S removal. Closely established by Finke/Denno-type results.
3. **F protected from D:** V changes D→F attack or functional response. This can offset or override hypotheses 1–2. Hossie/Murray is direct support for this alternative.
4. **Behavioral sheltering:** D cues cause F or S to change activity/refuge use, reducing S→F without D consuming S. Davenport/Chalcraft and Kishida et al. support versions of this route.

An original local approximation makes the focal sign transparent. Let total per-capita frog hazard be `H = a_D D + a_S(V) S(D)` at a specified time/density state. Assume `dS/dD < 0`, `da_S/dV < 0`, and temporarily hold `a_D` and `dS/dD` independent of V. Then `dH/dD = a_D + a_S(V) dS/dD`; the second term is the indirect benefit. Consequently `d²H/(dD dV) = (da_S/dV)(dS/dD) > 0`: vegetation strengthens marginal D harm **because it shrinks the value of removing S**. This is a deduction under assumptions, not a new result from these papers or proof that the real model satisfies all terms. If V also changes D→F, D→S, prey density or predator recruitment, the additional derivatives can change the sign.

Crucially, this deduction concerns the vegetation×D interaction. It does **not by itself prove the separate S×D amplification pattern or positive V×S relation**. All three need joint evaluation on the same physical and statistical scales.

## Discriminating predictions and model tests

| Contrast | F-from-S protection prediction | Alternative separated |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed F and S densities; no D; low/high V | S-caused per-capita F hazard falls with V. | If only S is protected from D, this direct contrast need not change. |
| Fixed S and D densities; remove F feeding feedback | V need not reduce D→S attack in the focal model. | S-refuge hypothesis specifically reduces D→S capture and raises S persistence. |
| Disable D→S consumption while preserving other links | The consumptive lost-benefit component disappears; any remaining V×D must have another source. | Direct D→F response, depletion and cue-mediated effects can remain. |
| Cue-only D versus freely feeding D | Consumptive S removal requires free D; behavioral rescue may occur with cues alone. | Separates density-mediated removal from Kishida/Davenport-type pathways. |
| Hold F density constant or replace consumed F; retain per-capita hazard | A genuine edge-mediated benefit loss can remain without a survival floor. | Depletion/response-curve amplification should change when density trajectories are clamped. |
| Reverse relative D/S sizes or reduce their temporal overlap | D→S-mediated benefit loss weakens when D cannot consume S; reciprocal links can change the outcome. | A stage-insensitive effect points toward direct F attack or another route. |
| Separate same-season refuge from emergence/recruitment subsidy | Immediate consumption changes versus delayed next-season D pressure. | Vegetation-assisted emergence is not tested merely by adding refuge coefficients. |

For the model, report cumulative consumption on every edge, predator survival, and frog hazard alongside the three interaction contrasts. A good fit to rank coefficients alone cannot distinguish these mechanisms. Use the same fitting recipe and prespecified predictors across candidates. A controlled edge deletion identifies necessity within that model, not necessity in nature.

For existing macro data, stratification by plausible breeding phenology, predator size classes or water persistence can test compatibility and reveal failures, but suitability does not identify actual co-occurrence, sizes or attacks. The focal and S-protection routes can generate similar broad patterns; absent direct mediator measurements, retain them as non-identified alternatives rather than naming a single proven cause.

## Manuscript claim ceiling

Suitable: “The simulations identify a conditional route by which protection from one predator reduces the indirect benefit of its suppression by another. This route is compatible with the observed vegetation-dependent association and can be distinguished from predator refuge and functional-response alternatives by edge-specific predictions.” Use “compatible” only for patterns actually passed in the current frozen run.

Avoid: “Vegetation newly shown to intensify predator effects”; “first demonstration of refuge-mediated indirect effects”; “calling patterns reveal salamander removal”; “all three macro interactions establish this mechanism”; or interpreting the existence of stage reversal as evidence that reversal is necessary. The strongest potential advance is an explicit, falsifiable **comparison of protected links and their joint observational signatures**, not novelty of indirect effects themselves. The present audit supplies neither empirical mediation identification nor a standalone basis for NEE readiness.
