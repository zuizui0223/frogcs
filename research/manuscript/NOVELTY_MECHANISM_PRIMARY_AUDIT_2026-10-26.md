# Novelty and mechanism audit: frog chorus, dragonflies, caudates, vegetation, and stage reversal

Audit date: 2026-10-26. Bounded primary-source audit of six close precedents plus one theory boundary check; not a systematic review or proof of priority. This memo evaluates the proposed framing, not the validity of local fitted results, which were not independently reanalysed here. Sources were inspected through publisher pages/PDFs and the original article abstract. All proposed novelty statements below are conditional on the actual analyses meeting their stated evidence gates.

## Verdict

The individual ingredients are established: a third predator can modify amphibian predator–prey relationships; dragonflies and newts can have asymmetric joint effects on tadpoles; habitat complexity can change pond intraguild predation; frog–dragonfly roles can reverse across life stages; and reversal can change population dynamics in mathematical models. The defensible contribution is their integration into a specifically defined empirical-to-model comparison: contextual associations in an adult frog chorus response, followed by explicit tests of which stage-resolved mechanisms could generate or fail to generate those associations. Neither a three-way regression term nor a multiyear simulation alone establishes a newly discovered ecological mechanism.

## Three distinct claims that must remain separate

1. **Statistical effect modification.** A dragonfly × caudate × vegetation term in a chorus model says that the fitted conditional association varies jointly across those predictors, on the model's specified link scale. It is not automatically evidence of consumption, competition, niche partitioning, or a change in a demographic interaction coefficient. Response-scale contrasts and uncertainty are needed because link functions can also create apparent nonadditivity.
2. **Mechanistic interaction modification.** A claim that caudates change a dragonfly's per-capita effect on frogs requires independent evidence for the relevant stages and process, preferably manipulations, measured attack/encounter rates, behaviour, or recruitment. The experimental distinctions in Davenport & Chalcraft and Kishida et al. are useful templates. A pairwise trophic network can also produce indirect population effects without an explicit higher-order coefficient.
3. **Model sufficiency.** A stage-structured model can demonstrate that stated assumptions suffice to generate a pattern or change an outcome. This supports a mechanism's plausibility within the model. It does not show that the mechanism operated at the observed sites, nor that the model's parameter values describe those sites.

These are audit interpretations of the designs above, not claims that the cited authors analysed this project's chorus data.

“Hierarchical” also requires definition. A nested statistical model, a sequence of contextual modifiers, and a trophic hierarchy are three different objects. None is synonymous with a mechanistic higher-order interaction. If the design only estimates context-dependent associations, “joint contextual dependence” or “statistical effect modification” is more precise.

An additional theory boundary check rules out novelty based on merely adding a third species to role reversal: Mortoja et al. (2024), *Role Reversals in a Tri-Trophic Prey–Predator Interaction System: A Model-Based Study Using Deterministic and Stochastic Approaches*, constructs a three-species system with reversal between intermediate and top predators and examines deterministic/stochastic dynamics. The publisher abstract and model discussion were inspected. Its stage illustration should not automatically be equated with independently tracked stage compartments. ## Narrow contribution to pursue

An appropriate prospective statement is:

> We evaluate whether the association between model-derived dragonfly environmental suitability and adult frog chorus activity depends jointly on caudate context and vegetation, and use stage-resolved multiyear models to examine how aquatic predation, emergence-stage role reversal, and habitat-dependent encounters can change demographic outcomes under stated assumptions.

Use “observed association” only after the relevant estimand and uncertainty are established. If the empirical three-way term is weak, report that weakness and frame the simulation as exploring conditions under which an effect might arise, rather than as explaining an observed mechanism. “Macro” needs a defined geographic and temporal sampling extent; pooled observations alone do not establish a macroecological scale. “Dragonfly niche” needs measured resources, habitat use, or functional traits; occurrence alone is insufficient to identify a niche mechanism.

The strongest conceptual emphasis would be the mismatch between a stage-aggregated field response and stage-specific interaction pathways: apparent frog–dragonfly associations need not map directly onto a single fixed predator–prey sign. This is an integration-based contribution, not a claim that ontogenetic reversal itself is newly recognized.

## Minimum evidence gates for the integrated argument

- Lock the observational response: chorus detections/activity, acoustic index, occupancy, or abundance are different estimands. Account for survey effort, season/weather and detection where the data allow; do not silently interpret chorus as population size or recruitment.
- Match taxonomic stages to mechanisms: adult dragonfly records do not directly measure larval predation pressure, and caudate records need species/stage resolution before assigning a role. Vegetation may represent emergence substrate, refuge, visibility, or shared habitat preference; distinguish these alternatives.
- Compare explicit model counterfactuals: no role reversal, reversal enabled, third-party pathway removed, and habitat modulation removed, with comparable initial conditions and parameter uncertainty. Avoid adding several pathways at once and attributing the resulting difference to just one.
- Separate within-year stage transitions from between-year recruitment/survival. Explain the pathway by which larval interactions could reach the adult chorus response, including temporal lags and dispersal assumptions.
- Keep novel predictions falsifiable: which stage, season, habitat context, and lag should strengthen, weaken, or reverse the association? A simulation that reproduces a qualitative pattern across unconstrained parameters is a scenario result, not validation.

## Search boundary

Searches targeted the four supplied DOIs, ecological higher-order interaction theory, and ontogenetic predator–prey reversal models. Six close sources and one additional theory boundary check were retained. This did not exhaust acoustic-monitoring literature, large-scale joint-distribution models, dragonfly habitat studies, or all stage-structured theory. Therefore “first”, “unprecedented”, and “previous studies ignored third-party/context/stage effects” are unsupported. Related acoustic/vegetation precedents are being audited separately by the parent task, and no priority is claimed for combining acoustic responses with biotic and habitat predictors. The memo identifies close precedents and a defensible positioning strategy; it does not establish global absence of the proposed combination.
