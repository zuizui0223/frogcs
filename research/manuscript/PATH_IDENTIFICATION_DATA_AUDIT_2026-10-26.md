## Priority 1: compare mechanisms in the existing fixed-pair frame

Use the already fixed strong-versus-silent pairs, keeping matching and route components unchanged. Construct a conditional logit using the chosen-minus-silent design differences. Form each endpoint product first and THEN take the difference (e.g., O_chosen*S_chosen - O_silent*S_silent, not delta_O*delta_S). Carry all lower-order terms. Standardize predictors consistently using a declared reference cohort.

Compare fixed models: (A) O*S*V; (B) add prior water metrics; (C) add strict same-season past calling frequency; (D) add stop-level air/noise where coverage permits. Report coefficients/contrasts and their joint uncertainty under route-component resampling, not predictive gain. Pair run is shared, so route-night-common weather is controlled by the conditioning, whereas local hearing conditions can still differ. Join stop observations by `(run, chosen_site)` and `(run, silent_site)` and assert row counts and uniqueness. Preserve the canonical route-component assignment from the fixed-pair data.

Interpretation: attenuation after water adjustment supports a shared habitat explanation; attenuation after history adjustment supports persistent site differences or historical use as competing explanations. Persistence strengthens the conditional association but cannot distinguish larval predation from adult habitat selection. History could itself mediate population feedback, so its adjustment must be a named alternative estimand, not an automatic best model. Environmental scores are themselves functions of environmental predictors; report common support/collinearity and avoid presenting residual scores as pure biotic effects.

Water coverage: 3-km mean water frequency and water-positive area fraction cover all 4,040 pairs; conditional frequency on water-positive pixels covers 3,907 (133 missing). The corresponding 1-km conditional frequency covers only 2,204. Source: `research/results/naamp_prior_water_20261026/matched_water_coverage.csv`. Use the all-pair quantities first, then explicit complete-case sensitivity. Annual water detection is not hydroperiod days.

## Priority 2: season and species specificity

Recompute call-frequency outcomes by source run/season or local date windows on the frozen site cohort, with explicit minimum visits and coverage. Compare O:S and O:V contrasts across windows using shared route bootstrap draws. Keep estimates for all windows rather than selecting the strongest. This tests whether the association is restricted to portions of the calling season. It does NOT measure larval-stage overlap: calling date is a breeding signal and local development timing remains unobserved.

`source_taxa` can support species-level any-call occurrence but cannot support species-level strong calling. In `research/scripts/naamp_three_guild_occurrence.py`, `naamp_history` stores `strong` from the maximum CallingIndex across taxa while `source_taxa` contains all taxa with CallingIndex 1, 2, or 3. Reconstruct taxon-specific CI>=2 from source `Counts.csv`, joined through `Stops.csv` and run metadata using the existing hash pins in `scripts/naamp/run_naamp_ecological_pulse.py`. Preserve surveyed-zero construction; absence of a Counts row cannot be used without the original completeness rules. Pool or list sparse taxa transparently. Do not use the same focal response to choose a favorable developmental window and then call the contrast confirmatory.

## Priority 3: negative-control guild contrasts and score robustness

Species-level suitability is available at `research/results/interaction_species_sdm_20261026/naamp_species_suitability.csv.gz` (`site`, `species`, `species_key`, `guild`, `background`, `suitability_percentile`). Pond-breeding classification is the five-name `POND` set in `naamp_site_use_associations.py`. Existing exploratory species correlations are only marginal correlations; they are not taxon-specific O:S:V fits.

Compare pond-breeding Caudata with an explicitly defined non-pond complement and leave-one-species-out pond scores under both backgrounds. These are specificity tests: a pond-life-history mechanism predicts stronger correspondence in the relevant group, whereas comparable patterns in non-pond taxa favor broad environmental/biogeographic structure. Non-pond is not a perfect causal negative control because geographic distributions differ. Preserve common support and do not equate significance differences with a statistically tested difference.

## Claim progression

The strongest attainable next statement is: "The modifier association persists (or attenuates) when comparing environmentally similar, same-night sites, accounting separately for preceding water conditions, past use and observation conditions, and shows (or lacks) seasonal/taxon specificity." That narrows competing explanations. It still does not observe the mediator nodes (larval predator abundance, risk response, refuge use, emergence success). Mechanism-model pathway deletion and exposure-gradient tests can then determine whether a candidate mechanism produces the same signed contrast, rather than merely any negative population response.

There are no repeated `(run, chosen_site, silent_site)` keys; 960 rows repeat a chosen/silent site pairing from another run. No physical site belongs to more than one `route_component`. Retain component resampling rather than treating 4,040 pairs as independent.

Within-pair suitability is exactly tied for 2,573 Odonata pairs under uniform background and 2,570 under target background; pond-Caudata ties number 2,577 and 2,576 respectively. Median absolute difference is zero for all four score types. The 75th/90th percentiles of absolute Odonata difference are 0.004302/0.020083 (uniform) and 0.002750/0.008083 (target). Matching deliberately restricts environmental variation and these SDM scores are coarse. Report effective nonzero contrasts, matrix rank and conditioning; an uncertain fit cannot by itself reject an ecological interaction. Interactions can vary through vegetation even when O and S tie, so also inspect individual interaction-column support.
