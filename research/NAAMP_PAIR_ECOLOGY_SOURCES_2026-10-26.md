# NAAMP pair selection from independent breeding and acoustic evidence

2026-10-26. Literature selection was made before inspecting new pair-specific NAAMP coefficients or selecting pairs by their signs. This note fixes the ecological candidates; the analysis contract must separately freeze sample, outcome, covariates, eligibility thresholds, and multiplicity handling. Primary studies and official agency accounts are used below. No exact frequency cutoffs are invented from common names or call descriptions.

## Selected candidates and their roles

The secondary pair is an overlap comparator, not a proven noninteracting control. Do not pool these three pairs into a single assumed competition coefficient. Both directions should be reported where estimable; published asymmetries do not license selecting the direction with the stronger result.

## 1. Green frog–bullfrog: direct evidence is at much finer time scales

Herrick, Wells, Farkas & Schultz (2018), *Noisy Neighbors: Acoustic Interference and Vocal Interactions between Two Syntopic Species of Ranid Frogs, Rana clamitans and Rana catesbeiana*, Journal of Herpetology 52:176–184, [DOI 10.1670/17-049](https://doi.org/10.1670/17-049). The [author-posted article text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324555504_Noisy_Neighbors_Acoustic_Interference_and_Vocal_Interactions_between_Two_Syntopic_Species_of_Ranid_Frogs_Rana_clamitans_and_Rana_catesbeiana) was inspected; the publisher DOI page was inaccessible to the browser used here.

The study describes overlapping breeding seasons and call frequencies. Bullfrog calls are longer and more frequent; green frogs place calls in gaps. Its focal-neighbor recordings are from 26 May–18 June, 21:00–01:00, and the whole-pond temporal analysis uses 100-ms intervals. This supports an acoustic-interference hypothesis, but does **not** predict that one species must be absent from an entire NAAMP listening visit or route when the other calls. A null/positive coarse association cannot refute fine-scale avoidance. Habitat sharing can produce positive co-occurrence while acoustic interference persists.

The underlying [Herrick (2013) dissertation](https://digitalcommons.lib.uconn.edu/dissertations/214/) additionally reports green-frog territories and eggs closer to shore and under heavier overhead cover than bullfrog counterparts. These habitat differences are potential confounders, not adjustment variables actually measured in NAAMP. The dissertation and 2018 article are linked work, not two independent confirmations.

## 2. Gray-treefrog pair: direct experimental interaction evidence, difficult identification

Reichert & Gerhardt (2014), *Behavioral strategies and signaling in interspecific aggressive interactions in gray tree frogs*, Behavioral Ecology 25:520–530, [DOI 10.1093/beheco/aru016](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/aru016). The [publisher abstract](https://oup.silverchair-cdn.com/article-minimal/511983) was inspected; full methods were not available through that endpoint.

Both species call alongside one another in ponds with similar call spectra. Playback and staged interactions elicited heterospecific aggression. Interspecific encounters escalated more often than intraspecific ones and more often ended with displacement. Neither species had an overall winning advantage. H. versicolor initiated physical contact more often; this does not establish a universal directional population effect. The abstract relates behavioral asymmetry to previous evidence of unequal costs of call overlap, but those prior experiments were not independently inspected here and are not a quantitative prior for our coefficient.

Official [Maryland DNR account](https://dnr.maryland.gov/wildlife/Pages/plants_wildlife/herps/Anura.aspx?FrogToadName=Cope%27s+Gray+Treefrog) states that trill rate differs but depends on temperature; chrysoscelis is more common on the Coastal Plain and versicolor elsewhere in Maryland. Thus geography, temperature, and observer identification can create apparent replacement. Treat pooled or ambiguous labels as an identification limitation; do not allocate them to both species or count them as two independent detections. The [Missouri Department of Conservation account](https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/gray-treefrog-copes-gray-treefrog) gives an early-April to early-July breeding period, sometimes extending into August.

## 3. Spring peeper–upland chorus frog: overlap comparator with weaker mechanism evidence

A primary natural-history note in [Catesbeiana 42(1), 2022, page 23](https://www.virginiaherpetologicalsociety.com/catesbeiana-pdf/cat42n1/cat42n1.pdf) reports audio records of both taxa together in Clarke County, Virginia, on 21–22 March 2021, with feriarum calling among peepers. This establishes local simultaneous calling, not spectral overlap, competition, or a range-wide effect.

The official [Montgomery Parks species accounts](https://montgomeryparks.org/caring-for-our-parks/wildlife/frogs/) list peeper calling from late February into early May and upland chorus frog calling from mid-February into April. They also warn that the peeper encounter call may resemble the upland chorus frog. The same source provides the Maryland summer windows in the table above. These are local phenological and identification guides, not estimated NAAMP phenology or nationwide climatic thresholds.

## Environmental and observation alternatives to a competition claim

- Shared wetland suitability and species-specific seasonal/weather responses can create co-calling. The primary observational study *Environmental factors influencing calling in sympatric anurans* [PubMed record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28466161/) reports different meteorological associations even for concurrently calling species in New Brunswick. Air/water temperature, rain, time of day, and season should not be represented by a single common response shared by every species.
- Stable site effects reduce persistent habitat confounding, but do not adjust unmeasured changes in water depth, vegetation, or local sound propagation. Route-night effects align broad weather and timing, but do not make the ten stops the same breeding pond.
- A same-visit negative association can reflect observer masking or identification competition. A lagged association avoids using the focal visit's sound as its sole predictor, but remains confounded by persistent habitat, phenology, prior effort, and imperfect detection. Neither alone identifies a biological effect of a competitor.
- Territorial displacement, suppression of calling, rapid alternation of notes, reproductive interference, and larval resource competition are distinct mechanisms. NAAMP visit-level call indices can test coarse behavioral patterns; they do not supply note timing, females' perception, breeding success, or larval competition.

## Candidates not promoted to the primary set

P. crucifer–gray treefrogs: some seasonal overlap is plausible, but the sources inspected here did not provide pair-specific interference evidence comparable to the two primary pairs. H. cinerea–H. squirella: available refuge studies concern different mechanisms and did not establish pair-specific call interference. Do not expand the primary family merely because these pairs are common or yield more favorable coefficients. Numeric spectral-overlap thresholds remain unestablished without a curated acoustic trait dataset; record this limit rather than infer frequency from descriptive calls.
