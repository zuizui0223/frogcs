# Illustrative two-versus-three guild delay experiment

2026-10-26. Secondary to the empirical occurrence pattern work. The existing yearly kernel model does not implement a growing predator's advantage over a late cohort, and a risk-path knockout does not remove the third population. This new minimal illustration directly varies those assumptions; it is not fit to NAAMP/GBIF/GloBI or a validated species model.

One season, normalized F tadpole cohort, S larval predator, D nymph predator. S starts at day0, D enters at day35 if present, F starts at day10+delay. F requires45 days to leave the aquatic stage. S predator size and F size grow at fixed declared rates; an assumed logistic size-dependent S attack vulnerability creates a potential later-cohort disadvantage. S and D densities follow simple logistic background dynamics plus small feeding gains. D also directly eats F; its risk cue divides S's attack rate by1+qD. No D density is present in the two-guild model. The direct-only three-guild control has D but q=0. Drying before F completes45 days gives zero aquatic completion. Vegetation refuge multiplies both direct F-loss terms by1-refuge.

Compare two guilds; three guilds/direct only; three guilds/direct plus inhibition. Delay0..60 every5 days; cue strengths0,1,3,10,30; D attack0.002,0.01,0.03; refuge0 or0.5; water persistence60,90,120 days. Report the whole grid, not only a rescue example; one illustrative panel uses water120/refuge0/D attack0.002/q10. Explicit Euler step0.1 days, positivity-safe survival update, numerical step-halving check. Outputs are hypothetical fraction completing aquatic development and trajectories, not empirical success, coexistence or fitted population trends. This does not model reciprocal F-adult→D-emergence predation or all stage mechanisms.
